# Exact angular certificate for the signed test

This appendix supplies the bounds used in Sec. 6 of the manuscript. The bandwidth is an all-energy, all-spin bound for the angular operator only; reconstruction of the chosen amplitude can require a smaller band.

## Profile and pointwise lower transform

Use the K=10 signed Bessel moment profile: B=normalized J_(23/2)(z)/z^(23/2) from the radius-one ball weight (1−r²)^10, and P the first ten even powers of 1/B². Average B(vk)²P((vk)²) over the beta(4,4) scales on [17/64,30/64], convolved with any C∞ positive unit mass supported in [−1/64,1/64]. Thus the inverse radial density g is smooth in its right radial variable and supported below r=31/32; put p=πg. It annihilates exactly the ten required null moments and has spectral transform H≥0. No artificial physical rescaling of a far-cutoff profile is used.

The exact replay certifies H(k)>1/256 for k≤21+45/400000 and H(k)>20/k⁴ for k≥20. Here are the inequalities used. B is positive and decreasing through z=14.1: B′ has opposite sign to J_(25/2), whose positive Riccati solution is convex through sqrt(156); up to 14.1 its potential is ≤1/4 and a constant-frequency comparison preserves positivity. Alternating rational Taylor bounds give B(11)>1/16, B(13)>1/60 and B(14.1)>1/160. The first bound gives the low-k assertion using v≤31/64. For k∈[20,32] use pre-mollifier scales [19,20]/64, with mass >.04 and final v≤21/64, so H k⁴>.04·20⁴/16²>20. For [32,40] and [40,45] use pre-scales [18,19]/64, mass >.012, and final v∈[17,20]/64; use B(13), B(14.1), respectively, and monotonicity of the positive polynomial P at z=8.5 and z=10.625, respectively. The resulting rational bounds exceed 20.

For k≥45 take scales v∈[11/32,7/16], with density >6/25 since the parent beta is bounded below on [21/64,29/64]. The Riccati-Bessel energy for u=√(πz/2)J_(23/2), potential ω²=1−132/z², satisfies ω²≤E≤1 there by the following elementary energy argument. With ω²=1−132/z²>0, the Bessel equation gives E′=(ω²)′u², and the usual large-z asymptotic gives E→1. Thus E≤1 and E′/E≤(ω²)′/ω² integrates to E≥ω². Since ω²>7/16 and interval length L≥135/32, integrating E=(uu′)′+2ω²u² gives average u²>1/32 (boundary absolute value ≤20/13). The top P coefficient obeys (23!!)^2 P_top>1600. Hence H k⁴>1600·(16/7)^4·(6/25)·(3/32)/32>20.

## Exact Gram geometry and spectral lower bound

Use the quaternion left frame of the S³ direction sphere, and embed each physical spin-two screen isometrically into the spatial ambient symmetric rank-two tensors of ℝ⁴. The pair embedding I_n is the tensor product of these embeddings and is polynomial of degree four in the ambient unit quaternion n. Define the ordered-pair Gram kernel G(n,m)=I_n* I_m. This is a positive-type kernel, not the nonpositive geodesic unitary-transport kernel. In the axial basis its spin pairing has the same rotation phases as the direct t pairing, but plane factor a=z instead of a_t. Its operator norm is ≤1 on real angles.

Let Θ be the cap angle at physical transfer cutoff Q, r=θ/Θ, k=(J+1)Θ. Multiplying G by the central radial profile g(r) is an exactly PSD operator: its quadratic form is the central convolution form applied to the embedded sections I_n f(n). The exact group harmonic coefficient of g(r), with Haar measure normalized to one, is Θ³/(2π²) times the Euclidean transform of g(r)sinc(Θr) at momentum (J+1)Θ. This transform is the average of H over a radius-Θ Euclidean momentum shell; harmonic positivity is exact, not a small-angle approximation. On an initial degree-J group component the polynomial multiplication I_n shifts ambient spherical degrees by at most four (tensor-product rule for S³ harmonics). Parseval and I_n*I_n=I therefore give a comparison lower bound for every right block by Θ³/(2π²) times the minimum H over |k′−k|≤5Θ. Thus for Θ≤9/400000 this lower is at least 1/256 for k≤21 and at least 19/k⁴ for k≥21, after dropping the common factor. It applies before restricting to Bose.

## Quantitative quadratic error

Write ρ(r)=q(Θr)/Q and A(r) for the normalized measure/Jacobian/Regge factor, as in the manuscript, and let T denote the exact weighted four-leg projector. In terms of p=πg the exact difference from the Gram test is

    E = h G + f D,     f=A p(ρ), h=f−p, D=T−G.

We state and derive the following concrete estimates for Θ≤9/400000; norms integrate in the rescaled cap, with the factor 4 from 4πg=4p left explicit below.

* Radial geometry: |ρ−r|≤Θ²r/10, ||ρ−r||_(C^4)≤Θ², ||A−1||_(C^4)≤2Θ². Indeed the expansions are ρ=r+Θ²r(1−r²)/24+O(Θ⁴) and A=1+Θ²(1/8+r²/2)+O(Θ⁴). On |Θ|≤1/16, |r|≤3/2 these functions (and ρ/r) have holomorphic norm <10, using the convergent sine/cosine series; the square root is its branch near 2. Evenness in Θ bounds the fourth-order remainder by 10·16⁴ Θ⁴/(1−(16Θ)²); up to four real r derivatives cost at most 4!·2⁴ by Cauchy. The leading coefficients and the tiny stipulated cap then give the displayed budgets.

* Matrix geometry: for m=0,…,4 and each Cartesian derivative of order m,
      ||∂^m D(Θrω)||_(scaled coordinate) ≤16 Θ² r^(2−m).
  The exact quadratic difference at axis θ is θ²[(C_axis⊗I+I⊗C_axis)/2+(F−B)/4], where the one-leg spin-two C_axis is the derivative of the paired tensor contraction in a at 1, ||C_axis||≤2. ## Bi-Laplacian and closure

In scaled normal coordinates the metric is sinc²(Θr) δ+(1−sinc²(Θr)) xx^T/r². The difference between its bi-Laplacian and the flat one satisfies the off-origin absolute norm estimate
    ||(Δ_Θ²−Δ_flat²)F|| ≤ 20000 Θ² Σ_(m=1)^4 r^(m−2) max_|α|=m ||∂^αF||.
For explicit coefficient bounds use inverse matrix with sinc^-2 in place of sinc², volume density sinc², and write the first-order Laplace coefficient as b_i=α^-1[r f′+4f]x_i with α=sinc² and f=(α−1)/r². The inverse metric minus identity and its first two partial derivatives have bounds 2Θ²r², 8Θ²r, 32Θ². The b coefficients similarly have bounds 8Θ²r, 32Θ² and 128Θ²/r (last deliberately weak). These follow termwise from the same sine series, or Taylor the even analytic multipliers in Θr; at this cap the next terms contribute less than 1% of the listed budgets. Substituting in (g^{ij}∂ij+b^i∂i)^2, summing at most 81 index terms per contraction, gives the estimate above. The metric difference does not alter the leading cusp delta since its top coefficient vanishes quadratically at the origin.

## Direct geometric matrix norm bound

The geometric derivative part of the conservative bandwidth can be bounded independently of the 25×25 entries. In complex normal coordinates |ξ_i|≤1/8, quaternion left multiplication has norm ≤exp(Σ|ξ_i|)≤exp(3/8)<3/2, and ||m−n||≤exp(3/8)−1<1/2. The scalar z=m_0 obeys |z−1|≤cosh(3/8)−1<0.08, by its power series. Thus |3+z²|>3.8 and energy-one momentum norms are <2.

On the actual physical vector screens, the Ward shift coefficient is, up to signs, [z/(3+z²)] e·m or [z/(3+z²)] e·n, by the same dot-product algebra as the explicit γ formula; the opposite leg on the same axis gives no term. For a unit input frame coefficient vector, the cross-dot magnitude is ≤(3/2)||m−n||<3/4, since the vector is transverse to its own axis. Consequently the gauge-shifted one-leg vector map has complex operator norm strictly below 3/2 + 2(1.08/3.8)(3/4) <2. Its spin-two tensor map has norm ≤4, and a contracted pair matrix has norm ≤16. Scalar |W_x|≤6 because |x_channel|≤8 and s²+t²+u²=8(3+z²) in the energy-one chart. The two ordinary pair operators each cost at most 16², and the Bell operator at most 5·16² using the Frobenius norm of a 5×5 paired matrix. So the full 25×25 holomorphic tensor has operator norm <2^14 throughout this polydisk. Vector-valued Cauchy on |ξ_i|≤1/16 therefore bounds every partial derivative through order four by 4!16^4·2^14 <2^35; after rescaling, the matrix C^4 error relative I is ≤2^37 Θ (maximum of component derivatives in operator norm, and the order-zero bound by the coordinate mean-value theorem).
