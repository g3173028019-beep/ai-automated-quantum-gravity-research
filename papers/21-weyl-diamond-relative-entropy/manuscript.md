# Coherent relative entropy of the Weyl algebra in a causal diamond

[Archive](../../README.md) · [Paper 21 in the index](../../docs/PAPER-INDEX.md#21-weyl-relative-entropy) · [Research map](../../docs/RESEARCH-MAP.md)

**Archive connections:** [09](../../papers/09-canonical-einstein-scalar-hadamard/README.md) · [18](../../papers/18-positive-boundary-sewing/README.md). 09 constructs positive physical Hadamard states in a coupled system; 18 separates positivity and sewing requirements in finite models. 21 instead starts with a prescribed vacuum and computes a local-algebra quantity. It provides neither a coupled-state construction nor a sewing theorem.

**Abstract.** In four-dimensional Minkowski vacuum, the double-cone algebra generated by the local linearized Weyl tensor has a gauge-invariant coherent relative entropy quadratic form. In concentric radial variables it is two scalar radial forms with the angular sectors \(\ell=0,1\) absent and with coordinates obtained from the electric and magnetic radial curvature components. The form cannot be represented by an integral of any regular finite-jet local curvature quadratic density, even with arbitrary smooth position-dependent coefficients and counterterms depending only on local edge jets. The proof uses compact admissible curvature data and a polynomial identity in the angular eigenvalue; it does not count a formal inverse transverse projector or missing local stress tensor as proof.

## 1. Physical normalization and an explicit curvature-to-canonical map

Take \(g=\eta+\kappa h\), \(\kappa^2=32\pi G\), \(\hbar=c=1\), with the two physical TT metric components of \(h\) normalized to the canonical massless action (polarization tensors orthonormal in the spatial Frobenius metric). Define the physical electric tensor \(E_{ij}=C^{(1)}_{0i0j}\) and the magnetic one with an orientation such that the field equations are \(\dot E=\operatorname{curl}B,\ \dot B=-\operatorname{curl}E\). The curl on symmetric spatial TT tensors can be taken in one index or symmetrized; they coincide on this class. Initial \(E,B\) are symmetric, tracefree and divergencefree, and these constraints are propagated by the two first-order equations. Signs of the magnetic dual or of the Riemann convention affect only coordinate signs in the result.

Let \(Y_{\ell m}\) be orthonormal scalar harmonics, \(\lambda=\ell(\ell+1)\), \(H_{AB}(Y)=D_AD_BY+(\lambda/2)\gamma_{AB}Y\), and let \(J\) rotate an oriented tangent plane by a right angle, acting symmetrically in one index of a tracefree symmetric tensor. For \(\ell\ge2\), the angular orthonormal tensor harmonics on the **momentum** unit sphere are
\[
 \mathcal Y^E_{AB}=H_{AB}(Y)/c_\ell,\qquad
 \mathcal Y^B_{AB}=JH_{AB}(Y)/c_\ell,\qquad
 c_\ell=\sqrt{\lambda(\lambda-2)/2}.
\]
The normalization follows from integration by parts and the unit-sphere Ricci tensor; \(\|H(Y)\|^2=c_\ell^2\), \(D_AD_B\mathcal Y^{E,AB}=c_\ell Y\), and the magnetic double divergence is zero. These harmonics span the momentum TT tensors and do not exist for \(\ell=0,1\).

A direct Fourier calculation fixes the reduced normalization without using TT metric potentials as locally supported observables. Write \(z=kr\), \(n=\mathbf x/r\), \(\hat k\) on the momentum unit sphere, \(\mu=n\cdot\hat k\). Because \(D_AD_B\mu=-\mu\gamma_{AB}\),
\[
 \mathcal Y^{AB}n_A n_B e^{iz\mu}
     =-z^{-2}\mathcal Y^{AB}D_A D_B e^{iz\mu}.
\]
Double integration by parts gives the electric transform \(-4\pi i^\ell c_\ell j_\ell(z)Y_{\ell m}(n)/z^2\), and zero for the magnetic harmonic. On a normalized positive-frequency plane mode \(E_{ij}=\kappa k^2h_{ij}/2\). Magnetic duality multiplies \(k^2h_{ij}\) by the orthogonal polarization rotation, so the \(B_{rr}\) projection similarly selects the other parity, with a fixed sign. Multiplication by \(r^3\) cancels \(z^{-2}\) and \(k^2\) and leaves precisely \(rj_\ell(kr)\). Thus, with either consistent overall orientation sign in each slot,
\[
 q_{E,\ell m}=\frac{2r^3}{\kappa c_\ell}(E_{rr})_{\ell m},\qquad
 q_{B,\ell m}=\frac{2r^3}{\kappa c_\ell}(B_{rr})_{\ell m},\qquad
 p_{A,\ell m}=\partial_t q_{A,\ell m}\quad(A=E,B).
 \tag{1}
\]
The factors in (1) follow in the canonical TT convention stated above; no Newton constant or angular factors are assigned by fitting a radial energy. To see the full canonical assertion, expand each \(k\)-dependent TT plane amplitude in the orthonormal \(\mathcal Y^{E/B}\) basis. Its two parity amplitudes have exactly the same one-particle measure \(k\,dk/[2(2\pi)^3]\), with the angular normalization, as scalar positive-frequency plane amplitudes in the orthonormal \(Y_{\ell m}\) basis. The transform just computed gives the usual scalar spherical Bessel mode expansion for \(q/r\) and positive frequency unchanged; any common \(i^\ell\) or orientation signs are unit phases. Hence this is a real symplectic and vacuum-positive-frequency identification, and the canonical scalar equation and commutators hold with potential \(\lambda/r^2\). It is an isomorphism with two scalar radial mode sums, not two full scalar local nets at arbitrary translated spatial regions.

## 2. Equality of the actual concentric local algebras

One needs more than the reduced spectrum: the curvature net on a centered ball must be identified without a spatial TT projection through its boundary. Here is a direct reconstruction. For one real angular harmonic expand any of \(E,B\) in orthonormal radial/angular components:
\[
 T_{rr}=aY,\quad T_{rA}=b_eD_A Y+b_o\epsilon_A{}^B D_BY,\quad
 T_{AB}=-a\gamma_{AB}Y/2+c_eH_{AB}+c_o(JH)_{AB}.
\]
The flat divergence constraint gives
\[
 b_e=(r\partial_r+3)a/\lambda,\quad
 c_e=[2(r\partial_r+3)b_e-a]/(\lambda-2),\quad
 c_o=2(r\partial_r+3)b_o/(\lambda-2).
 \tag{2}
\]
For example in coordinate angular indices \(T^{\rm coord}_{rA}=rT_{rA}\) and \(T^{\rm coord}_{AB}=r^2T_{AB}\), so the tangential divergence contains \(r b'_e+3b_e+D^BT_{BA}\), and \(D^BH_{BA}=-(\lambda-2)D_AY/2\). The complementary odd vector coefficient \(b_o\) of \(E\) is fixed up to the curl sign by \(r\partial_t(B_{rr})_{\ell m}/\lambda\); the odd part of \(B\) is similarly fixed by \(\partial_t E_{rr}\). This follows from the radial component of the curl, which acts tangentially on \(T_{rA}\) and kills its gradient part. Equations (1)–(2) therefore reconstruct all initial gauge-invariant curvature from the two canonical radial pairs with **no new integration constant**, and conversely. Only angular projection/reconstruction on a sphere and radial differentials occur. In particular supports on open annuli are preserved. Low modes are absent on the physical plane-wave Hilbert space, not treated as extra edge oscillators. The origin condition is the regular spherical Bessel one, with \(q=O(r^{\ell+1})\) on smooth finite-multipole data. Allowing singular mass/dipole Coulomb data or boundary defects would define a different algebra.

For the ball \(r<R\), both directions of this map are localized in the ball: smearing any one harmonic entails the entire sphere at its given radius but never includes \(r\ge R\). Compact interior radial canonical coordinate and momentum tests are limits of curvature tests supported at times near zero in the double cone: smearing \(E_{rr},B_{rr}\) in time gives the former, and differentiating the time test approximates the latter. Conversely the differential reconstruction and finite propagation of the radial scalar and curvature equations put every corresponding spacetime smear in the same radial time-slice subspace. The equal-time initial-data description follows from the free hyperbolic time-slice identity; derivatives here are distributional smeared fields, not unsmeared insertions. One can first avoid \(r=0\) and then remove a cutoff, without assuming a sharp equal-time curvature cutoff is continuous in a critical Sobolev norm. For smooth spacetime curvature tests, the deleted tube of radius ε has spatial volume \(O(\epsilon^3)\); integrating the test in time by parts yields an on-shell Fourier bound \(C_N\epsilon^3(1+k)^{-N}\), which dominates the polynomial Weyl field factors. The deleted tube therefore has vanishing one-particle norm. For scalar time-slice coordinate/momentum tests, the smooth-origin annular approximation is dense in the usual \(H^{1/2}\oplus H^{-1/2}\) Cauchy norms (the point has zero capacity; the elementary scaled smooth cutoff is already sufficient). General smooth tests follow by angular harmonic limits in that norm since rotations act unitarily and smooth smearings have rapidly decaying angular tails. Thus the standard real local subspace, and its Weyl algebra with the vacuum, is exactly the direct sum of the two scalar radial standard subspaces in \(\ell\ge2\). This is a concentric-ball statement for the genuine curvature algebra; it makes no coordinate-TT factorization assumption across an arbitrary cut.

## 3. Coherent entropy as an explicit curvature quadratic form

For a massless conformal scalar in a ball use the vacuum relative entropy formula of [Longo–Morsella, arXiv:2012.00565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00565), Theorem 5.4 (unit radius), scaled to radius \(R\). With
\[
 f_R(r)=\frac{R^2-r^2}{2R},
\]
its coherent classical form in four spacetime dimensions is
\(\pi\int_{r<R}[f_R(\dot\phi^2+|\nabla\phi|^2)+\phi^2/R]\,d^3x\).
The coefficient of the last term may also be checked by differentiating the conformal scalar action of the ball modular vector on Cauchy data: the generator sends \(\phi\) to \(f_R\dot\phi\) and \(\dot\phi\) to \(\nabla\cdot(f_R\nabla\phi)-\phi/R\), before the factor \(2\pi\). For \(\phi=q(r)Y_{\ell m}/r\) the cross term in \(f_R(\partial_rq-q/r)^2\) integrates to \(f'_R q^2/r=-q^2/R\) after cancellation of its \(f_R/r^2\) part. The origin boundary vanishes by regularity and the rim boundary by \(f_R(R)=0\). This cancels the extra scalar \(\phi^2/R\) exactly. Equality of the vacuum standard subspaces established above and additivity on independent radial channels now give
\[
 S_{D_R}(E,B)=\pi\sum_{A=E,B}\sum_{\ell\ge2,m}\int_0^R dr\, f_R(r)
  \left[|p_{A,\ell m}|^2+|\partial_r q_{A,\ell m}|^2
                 +\frac{\lambda}{r^2}|q_{A,\ell m}|^2\right].
 \tag{3}
\]
This is the vacuum-relative entropy of the normalized coherent state, not an area-law vacuum entropy. It holds directly for smooth finite angular sums with regular compact Cauchy data, in particular the annular physical packets below. For the smooth finite-form closure, the same result is the sum of the nonnegative channel forms: equivalently take the increasing finite-channel Weyl algebras and use monotone continuity of relative entropy and the direct-sum one-particle modular operator. Data with divergent sum have infinite relative entropy. Smooth compact curvature test packets on the physical plane-wave Hilbert space may be treated by their smooth radial scalar profiles; there is no extra rim term in (3). Operationally one first extracts the local radial curvature coefficients, applies the factor \(c_\ell^{-1}\) in (1) and the time-curl relation, and then uses the explicit positive quadratic form. This supplies the angular Green-function weights \(1/[\lambda(\lambda-2)]\) and all radial weights; it requires no assertion that curvature jets alone at one spatial point determine the form.

## 4. A polynomial obstruction on exact admissible curvature data

A priori the denominators in (1) do not alone prove nonlocality: constraints can induce inverse angular factors even in a *local* curvature density. The following comparison avoids this mistake. Suppose (3) could be represented, for all smooth radiative data, by a ball integral of a regular finite-jet local curvature density plus a piece depending only on edge jets. At quadratic order about the vacuum its polarized form is a finite-order differential bilinear on \(E,B\) with smooth coefficient functions of position and the fixed ball/background. Any finite time derivatives are replaced by spatial curls using the physical equations. We test on data compactly supported in an interior annulus on the initial slice so that every rim jet vanishes, including time jets by the equations. Rotating the proposed density around the ball center and averaging over rotations preserves its locality and finite order and the value of the proposed entropy. The averaged coefficients in a spherical description are radial and the angular operations are rotation invariant.

Let \(u(r)\) be any real compact smooth radial function in that annulus. For every physical \(\ell\ge2\), take \(B=0\) and one pure even \(E\) reconstructed by (2) with
\[
 a=\lambda(\lambda-2)u,\qquad
 b_e=(\lambda-2)(r\partial_r+3)u,\qquad
 c_e=[2(r\partial_r+3)^2-\lambda]u,\qquad b_o=c_o=0.
 \tag{4}
\]
These are exact divergence-free STF initial data, supported in the same annulus, and yield smooth finite-energy radiative coherent states. For example the TT metric Cauchy data with \(B=0\) can be reconstructed by inverting the ordinary Laplacian on \(E\) and choosing zero initial TT velocity; its low-frequency behavior is harmless for compact smooth TT curvature (there is no constant transverse tensor at zero momentum in the Fourier transform by transversality). Alternatively (1)–(2) directly give compact smooth radial canonical data, with regularity at the center, defining the physical state. The second radial canonical position is zero and both momenta vanish: the only angular vector of \(E\) is a gradient, so the radial curl of \(E\) is zero.

The proposed local bilinear on (4) is polynomial in \(\lambda\), as a radial differential form. More precisely, any angular contraction integrated over the sphere between scalar, vector or STF tensor descendants of \(Y\) defines, after taking formal angular adjoints, an invariant scalar differential operator on the unit sphere. Such an operator is polynomial in \(-\Delta_{S^2}\): rotation invariance of the principal symbol at a point makes it a power of the metric norm of momentum (no nonzero odd highest symbol), and subtraction of the corresponding Laplacian power lowers its order to prove the assertion by induction. This proof includes angular epsilon tensors and the radial/angular connection terms obtained from rewriting Cartesian derivatives in spherical coordinates. Integration by parts in the annulus converts the radial form to a formally symmetric radial differential operator, still polynomial in \(\lambda\).

By contrast, substituting (4) into (1) and (3) gives
\[
 S_{D_R}(u)=\frac{8\pi}{\kappa^2}\lambda(\lambda-2)
 \int dr\, f_R\left[(\partial_r(r^3u))^2+\lambda r^4u^2\right].
 \tag{5}
\]
Thus an asserted equality on all physical \(\ell\ge2\) is an equality of polynomial radial forms on every compact annular \(u\): for each test pair all polynomial coefficients agree by infinitely many physical \(\lambda\)'s. Divide the polynomial by \(\lambda-2\) and evaluate it formally at \(2\). This does **not** add an unphysical \(\ell=1\) state.

On the local side only the STF-tensor-times-STF-tensor contributions from \(c_e\) can survive that test. The scalar/trace and gradient-vector amplitudes in (4) individually have a factor \(\lambda-2\), so their mutual terms have a double zero. For any mixed term against \(c_e\), the angular polynomial also vanishes at \(\lambda=2\), since \(H_{AB}(Y)=0\) for every genuine \(\ell=1\) spherical harmonic. This supplies the second zero even if derivatives act on any angular slot. The tensor-tensor angular coefficient has at least the same single zero, and its residue can be any finite radial bilinear in \(c_e\) and radial derivatives. Consequently the putative local residue has radial Euler operator \(D^*KD\) for some finite-order formally symmetric scalar radial differential operator \(K\) with smooth coefficients, where
\[
 D=2[(r\partial_r+3)^2-1].
\]
Measure factors may be absorbed into \(K\) and its formal adjoint. The operator \(D\) is genuinely second order on the annulus. If \(K\ne0\) on any open subinterval where its leading coefficient does not vanish, the order of \(D^*KD\) is the order of \(K\) plus four, because the scalar highest-symbol product is nonzero there. If the local operator residue vanishes, it cannot match a nonzero form either. The residue of (5), however, has strictly nonzero differential order **two** on that annulus, as \(f_R>0\) and \(r>0\). This is a contradiction.

This test is not a mere scaling estimate or an assertion that isolated denominators cannot emerge from local constrained invariants. It evaluates a necessary polynomial identity on all exact physical \(\ell\ge2\) data, with a radial differential-order contradiction, and is unaffected by edge jets, total divergences or constraint terms. It excludes regular finite-jet local representations of the curvature quadratic form, not nonlocal relational energy operators, additional edge degrees of freedom, or a description using potentials chosen in a ball. A nonlinear finite-jet functional with a well-defined quadratic Taylor coefficient would face the same obstruction.

## Literature and scope

[Benedetti–Casini, arXiv:1908.01800](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.01800), §§5–6, supply the two scalar isospectral sphere channels and their low-mode truncation for entropy calculations. [Longo–Morinelli, arXiv:1806.04967](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.04967) treats higher-helicity double-cone algebras and [Longo–Morsella](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00565) the scalar coherent entropy; [arXiv:2401.02345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02345) concerns a different general entropy bound. The contribution of this work is the explicitly normalized curvature quadratic form and the finite-jet locality test on compact physical data. It is limited to linearized radiative gravity in the Minkowski vacuum and a centered double cone. It is not a definition of nonlinear local gravitational energy.
