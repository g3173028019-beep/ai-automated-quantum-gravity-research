\begin{abstract}
Boundary and reconstruction approaches to quantum gravity often formulate strong
requirements---positivity, gauge reduction, causal propagation, sewing, state
selection, and semiclassical recovery---without exhibiting a single continuum sector
in which the requirements can be checked with compatible domains and conventions.
This paper constructs a deliberately narrower benchmark.  We start from the
Einstein--scalar action with negative cosmological constant, expand about the
zero-scalar AdS$_4$ background, and work on a causally convex globally hyperbolic
interior double cone whose closure is disjoint from the timelike conformal boundary.
The classical nonminimal BV datum, its gauge-fixed tangent complex, and the exact
de Donder ghost operator are fixed explicitly.  Using source-specialized results on
linearized Einstein--Klein--Gordon theory and a derived block inversion, we obtain a
full advanced/retarded Green matrix for $(h,\phi,c,\bar c,b)$ for every real auxiliary
gauge parameter, including the nonschur value zero.

For a refined double cone we embed the local geometry into a spatially compact
Fuchsian locally-AdS host.  Published Hadamard existence theorems, a Fredholm
left-inverse construction, and an explicit normalization transport then yield a
five-field BRST-compatible Hadamard system and a positive, no-extra-null
ghost-number-zero one-particle space.  The free functional algebra is built on the
equicausal, rather than unrestricted microcausal, domain.  It satisfies the exact
graded Ward identity, Einstein causality, and a fixed-ambient time-slice property.
We also prove a sharp limitation: the ordinary field-algebra dagger does not descend
to the same BRST cohomology in the frozen nonminimal reality convention.  Finally, the
first $h\phi\phi$ matter deformation and its scalar-antifield completion form an exact
classical first-order BV subcocycle; a conserved renormalized one-insertion stress
normalization is available, while the associated local AMWI/QME obstruction class is
typed but not computed.

The construction is a benchmark, not a derivation of gravity.  It assumes the
Einstein--scalar action.  It supplies neither global reflecting AdS boundary
conditions, a full interacting quantum master equation, an operational boundary
instrument, a regulator-removal theorem, nor an emergent infrared bridge.  The paper
separates source-specialized inputs from project-level compositions throughout and
states an exact stop line for further promotion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Why a benchmark is needed}

The phrase ``boundary reconstruction'' covers programs with rather different
ontologies.  Some begin with amplitudes assigned to codimension-one boundaries, some
with nets of observables, some with constraint complexes, and some with positive
functionals or moment data.  Despite these differences, a mature continuum sector has
to make several structures coexist: a gauge-reduced classical phase space, causal
propagation, a quantum algebra, an admissible state, a physical positivity statement,
and sufficiently controlled interactions.  It is easy to verify each item in a
different model or convention.  It is much harder to exhibit them on one domain and
to state precisely which step fails when one attempts to pass from a local effective
field theory to a gravitational boundary theory.

The purpose of this paper is to provide such a controlled test object.  We call it the
\emph{local gauge benchmark}.  It is local, perturbative, and effective.  Its starting point
is not an unknown microscopic boundary amplitude but the familiar Einstein--scalar
action.  Consequently, success of the benchmark demonstrates compatibility of a
reconstruction architecture with a standard local gravitational sector; it cannot be
used to claim that Einstein gravity has been derived from that architecture.

The main technical issue is not writing the classical BV action.  The difficulty is
to keep the following chain typed and normalized:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \text{BV datum}&\longrightarrow \text{Green matrix}
 \longrightarrow \text{Hadamard pair}
 \longrightarrow \text{equicausal star algebra}\\
 &\longrightarrow \text{BRST reduction}
 \longrightarrow \text{time slice}
 \longrightarrow \text{first interaction test}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:chain}
\end{equation}
Gauge fields, ghosts, antighosts, the Nakanishi--Lautrup field, source pairings,
left/right derivatives, and the rescaling between geometric and source conventions all
matter.  A missing sign or an unjustified restriction of a covariance can break the
Ward identity or the physical inner product.

\subsection{Relation to established work}

The classical functional BV framework and its locally covariant gravity
specialization are due to Fredenhagen, Rejzner, Brunetti and collaborators
\cite{FredenhagenRejzner2012,BrunettiFredenhagenRejzner2016}.  Gauge-invariant
quantization of linearized Einstein--Klein--Gordon systems and linearized gravity on
globally hyperbolic backgrounds was developed in
\cite{Hack2014,FewsterHunt2013,HackSchenkel2013}.  Green-hyperbolic operators and
their restriction properties are treated systematically in \cite{Baer2015}.
The BRST phase-space and Hadamard criteria used below come from
\cite{WrochnaZahn2015}; the nontrivial gravity-state input is G\'erard's existence
theorem on Einstein spacetimes with compact Cauchy surfaces \cite{Gerard2023}.

Two corrections to older pAQFT shorthand are essential.  First, the traditional
unrestricted class of microcausal functionals is not closed under the Peierls bracket.
The equicausal domain introduced by Hawkins, Rejzner, and Visser repairs this defect and
supports the star product and time-slice homotopy \cite{HawkinsRejznerVisser2023}.
Second, a positive state on the reduced bosonic algebra is not by itself a full
nonminimal BRST state.  We therefore construct the tensor/vector Ward pair on a host,
transport it through the full five-field Green matrix, and prove the local
no-extra-null statement separately.

\paragraph{Scope ceiling.}
Every theorem below concerns a formal perturbative expansion on an interior globally
hyperbolic region.  There is no claim of a global AdS Hilbert space, a nonperturbative
or ultraviolet-complete theory, an independently functorial physical net, or an
observable boundary readout.  The strongest interacting statement is a first classical
matter subcocycle plus a typed but uncomputed quantum anomaly class.

\section{Local geometric and dynamical datum}

\subsection{The interior domain}

Let $(\widetilde{\mathrm{AdS}}_4,\gbar)$ be the universal cover of four-dimensional
anti-de Sitter spacetime with radius $L$ and mostly-plus signature.  In global
coordinates,
\begin{equation}
 \dd s^2_{\gbar}=\frac{L^2}{\cos^2\rho}
 \left(-\dd t^2+\dd\rho^2+\sin^2\rho\,\dd\Omega_2^2\right),
 \qquad
 \bar R_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{3}{L^2}\gbar_{\mu\nu},
 \label{eq:adsmetric}
\end{equation}
so that $\bar R=-12/L^2$, $\bar C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=0$, and
$\Lambda=-3/L^2$.

Global AdS has a timelike conformal boundary and is not globally hyperbolic without a
boundary prescription.  We therefore choose a connected, causally convex, globally
hyperbolic double cone $\U$ with compact closure contained in the interior of
$\widetilde{\mathrm{AdS}}_4$.  The closure is disjoint from the conformal boundary.
All proper gauge parameters, test sections, switching functions, and functional
derivatives used in integrations by parts have compact support in $\U$.

This restriction is structural, not cosmetic.  It makes the standard advanced and
retarded problem meaningful and removes conformal-boundary terms, but it also means
that no conclusion below is a theorem about reflecting global AdS.

\subsection{Einstein--scalar action and background}

Fix
\begin{equation}
 \kappa^2=32\pi G,
 \qquad m^2L^2=-2,
 \qquad \bar\phi=0,
 \label{eq:params}
\end{equation}
and use the standard-quantization label $\Delta_+=2$ for later comparison with an AdS
boundary problem.  The mass lies above the AdS$_4$ Breitenlohner--Freedman bound
$m^2L^2\geq-9/4$ \cite{BreitenlohnerFreedman1982}.  On the local interior domain the
label $\Delta_+$ does not impose a boundary condition.

The classical action is
\begin{align}
 S_{\mathrm{cl}}[g,\phi]
 &=\frac{2}{\kappa^2}\int_{\U}(R(g)-2\Lambda)\,\dd\mu_g \notag\\
 &\quad-\frac12\int_{\U}
 \left(g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi+m^2\phi^2\right)\dd\mu_g.
 \label{eq:classicalaction}
\end{align}
Compactly supported variation gives
\begin{equation}
 G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{\kappa^2}{4}T_{\mu\nu},
 \qquad (\Box_g-m^2)\phi=0,
 \label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 T_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi
 -\frac12g_{\mu\nu}\bigl((\nabla\phi)^2+m^2\phi^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
The background $(\gbar,0)$ solves \cref{eq:eom}.

Write
\begin{equation}
 g=\gbar+\kappa h.
 \label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
At free order the scalar block is
\begin{equation}
 P_\phi=\bar\Box-m^2=\bar\Box+\frac{2}{L^2}.
 \label{eq:scalarP}
\end{equation}
The normalization in \cref{eq:split} is retained throughout: a canonical graviton
backreaction of order $\kappa\hbar$ corresponds to a physical metric shift of order
$\kappa^2\hbar$.

\section{Classical BV complex and free tangent theory}

\subsection{Nonminimal fields and antibracket}

The nonminimal field multiplet is
\begin{equation}
 \Phi=(g_{\mu\nu},\phi,c^\mu,\cbar_\mu,b_\mu),
\end{equation}
with ghost numbers $0,0,1,-1,0$ and parities even, even, odd, odd, even.  For every
field $\Phi^A$ introduce a density-valued antifield $\Phi_A^\ddagger$ of ghost number
$-1-\gh(\Phi^A)$ and opposite parity.  Our antibracket is
\begin{equation}
 (F,G)=\sum_A\int_{\U}\left(
 \frac{\delta_rF}{\delta\Phi^A}\frac{\delta_lG}{\delta\Phi_A^\ddagger}
 -\frac{\delta_rF}{\delta\Phi_A^\ddagger}\frac{\delta_lG}{\delta\Phi^A}
 \right).
 \label{eq:antibracket}
\end{equation}
The BRST differential acts from the right Hamiltonian convention,
$sF=(F,S_{\mathrm{BV}})$.

Freeze
\begin{equation}
 sg=\mathcal L_cg,
 \quad s\phi=\mathcal L_c\phi,
 \quad sc=\frac12[c,c]_{\mathrm{gr}},
 \quad s\cbar=b,
 \quad sb=0,
 \label{eq:brstfull}
\end{equation}
where $[c,c]^\mu_{\mathrm{gr}}=2c^\nu\nabla_\nu c^\mu$.  The nonminimal master action is
\begin{align}
 S_{\mathrm{BV}}=S_{\mathrm{cl}}+
 \int_{\U}\bigl(&g^{\ddagger\mu\nu}\mathcal L_cg_{\mu\nu}
 +\phi^\ddagger\mathcal L_c\phi
 +\tfrac12c^\ddagger_\mu[c,c]^\mu_{\mathrm{gr}}
 +\cbar^{\ddagger\mu}b_\mu\bigr).
 \label{eq:bvaction}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Classical master equation; source-specialized]
Under the compact-support convention on $\U$,
\begin{equation}
 (S_{\mathrm{BV}},S_{\mathrm{BV}})=0,
 \qquad s^2=0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Diffeomorphism invariance of \cref{eq:classicalaction} holds without a boundary term for
compactly supported gauge parameters.  Lie derivatives close under the graded
vector-field bracket, whose graded Jacobi identity proves nilpotence on $g,\phi,c$.
The pair $(\cbar,b)$ is contractible.  In the canonical antibracket these statements
are equivalent to the classical master equation.  This is the standard BV implication
\cite{BatalinVilkovisky1981,FredenhagenRejzner2012} specialized to the present action.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Gauge fixing and tangent complex}

Rescale the full ghost by $c_{\mathrm{full}}=\kappa c$ and the corresponding antifields
canonically.  The free differential is
\begin{equation}
 s_0h_{\mu\nu}=2\bar\nabla_{(\mu}c_{\nu)},
 \qquad s_0\phi=0,
 \qquad s_0c=0,
 \qquad s_0\cbar=b,
 \qquad s_0b=0.
 \label{eq:s0}
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
 F_\mu(h)=\bar\nabla^\nu h_{\mu\nu}-\frac12\bar\nabla_\mu h,
 \qquad
 \Psi=\int_{\U}\cbar^\mu\left(F_\mu(h)+\frac{\lambda}{2}b_\mu\right)\dd\mu_{\gbar},
 \label{eq:gaugefermion}
\end{equation}
with real constant $\lambda$.  If $P_0$ is the ungauge-fixed Euler--Lagrange Hessian
and
\begin{equation}
 K\xi=(2\bar\nabla_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)},0),
\end{equation}
then the compactly supported tangent complex is
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_c(T\U)\xrightarrow{K}
 \Gamma_c(S^2T^*\U\oplus\mathbb R)
 \xrightarrow{P_0}
 \Gamma_c(S^2T^*\U\oplus\mathbb R)^*
 \xrightarrow{K^*}\Gamma_c(T\U)^*.
 \label{eq:tangentcomplex}
\end{equation}
Gauge invariance gives $P_0K=0$ and $K^*P_0=0$.

With $[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho=R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma$, the de
Donder ghost block is
\begin{equation}
 (P_{\mathrm{gh}}c)_\mu=\bar\Box c_\mu+\bar R_\mu{}^\nu c_\nu
 =\left(\bar\Box-\frac{3}{L^2}\right)c_\mu.
 \label{eq:ghostP}
\end{equation}
At the principal-symbol level, $F(k)K(k)c=k^2c$.  This identity checks gauge fixing
of pure-gauge directions; it is not a derivation of the complete lower-order graviton
Hessian.

\subsection{Free cocycles and their limited meaning}

Because the background Weyl tensor and scalar vanish,
\begin{equation}
 s_0C^{(1)}[h]=0,
 \qquad s_0\phi=0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the gauge change of a linearized natural tensor is the Lie derivative of its
background value.  Compactly smeared linearized Weyl and scalar observables therefore
give free ghost-number-zero cocycles.  They do not classify all cohomology classes.
Nor do they make the point fields $h_{\mu\nu}(x)$ or $\phi(x)$ nonlinear gravitational
observables.  A nonlinear relational dressing is a later, absent construction.

\section{The full nonminimal Green matrix}

\subsection{Source variables and convention map}

The normally hyperbolic source formulation of the linearized Einstein--Klein--Gordon
system in \cite{Hack2014} is particularly convenient.  Let
$\Theta=(\theta,\zeta)$ denote the trace-reversed source variables and define
\begin{equation}
 \cA(h,\phi)=\left(\frac{\kappa}{4}
 \left(h_{\mu\nu}-\frac12\gbar_{\mu\nu}h\right),\frac{\kappa}{2}\phi\right).
 \label{eq:Amap}
\end{equation}
Write $\bar K$ for the source gauge generator and $D=\bar K^\dagger$.  On the
zero-scalar Einstein background set
\begin{equation}
 R=D\bar K=-\frac12(\bar\Box+\mathrm{Ric}),
 \qquad Q=2R,
 \qquad \widetilde P=\bar P+2\bar K D.
 \label{eq:sourceops}
\end{equation}
Both $\widetilde P$ and $R$ are normally hyperbolic up to the nonzero scalar factor in
$R$.  Let $E_\pm$ and $C_\pm$ be their retarded/advanced Green operators, with the
sign convention $\supp G_\pm f\subset J^\pm(\supp f)$.

The source and local benchmark quadratic actions obey
\begin{equation}
 P_{0,\mathrm{b}}=-\frac{4}{\kappa^2}\cA^\dagger\bar P\cA.
 \label{eq:signmap}
\end{equation}
The minus sign is forced by the source action convention and reconciles the scalar
equation $\bar\Box-m^2$ with the source block $-\bar\Box+m^2$.

\subsection{Euler matrix}

In field and Euler-row order $(H,c,\cbar,b)$, with $H=(h,\phi)$, the gauge-fixed free
operator is
\begin{equation}
 P_{\mathrm{nm},\lambda}^{\mathrm{b}}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 P_0&0&0&F^\dagger\\
 0&0&M^\dagger&0\\
 0&-M&0&0\\
 F&0&0&\lambda I
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad M=FK=\bar\Box+\mathrm{Ric}.
 \label{eq:PnmLB}
\end{equation}
In grouped source variables $(\Theta,\beta;\eta,\bar\eta)$ the even and odd blocks are
\begin{equation}
 B_\lambda=
 \begin{pmatrix}-\bar P&\bar K\\D&\lambda I\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 O=\begin{pmatrix}0&-R^\dagger\\R&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:sourceblocks}
\end{equation}
The full matrix is graded formally self-adjoint.  Antifields generate the BV
differential but are not additional propagating rows of this field Hessian.

To define a supported square, let $C_{+,c}$ be the retarded map on compact sources and
$C_{+,pc}$ its canonical extension to past-compact sections; similarly use
$C_{-,c}$ and $C_{-,fc}$.  Put
\begin{equation}
 C_+^2=C_{+,pc}C_{+,c},
 \qquad C_-^2=C_{-,fc}C_{-,c}.
 \label{eq:C2}
\end{equation}
Then $RC_\pm^2=C_\pm$ and $C_\pm^2R=C_\pm$ on compact sections, and the causal
support orientation is preserved \cite{Baer2015}.

\begin{theorem}[Full Green matrix for every real $\lambda$; derived]
The even Green operators are
\begin{equation}
 G_\pm^{(B_\lambda)}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -E_\pm+\dfrac{1-2\lambda}{2}\bar K C_\pm^2D&\bar K C_\pm\\[2mm]
 C_\pm D&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \label{eq:evenGreen}
\end{equation}
and the odd Green operators are
\begin{equation}
 G_\pm^{(O)}=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&C_\pm\\-C_\pm&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:oddGreen}
\end{equation}
Together they are two-sided advanced/retarded Green operators for the source matrix.
No division by $\lambda$ occurs, so the statement includes $\lambda=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The gauge identities imply
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde P\bar K=2\bar K R,
 \qquad D\widetilde P=2RD.
\end{equation}
Uniqueness of supported Green solutions therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 E_\pm\bar K=\frac12\bar K C_\pm,
 \qquad DE_\pm=\frac12C_\pm D,
 \qquad
 \bar P E_\pm=E_\pm\bar P=1-\bar K C_\pm D.
 \label{eq:intertwine}
\end{equation}
To solve $B_\lambda(\Theta,\beta)=(f,g)$, apply $D$ to the first row.  One obtains
$R\beta=Df$, hence $\beta=C_\pm Df$.  The second row gives
$D\Theta=g-\lambda\beta$, and substitution yields
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde P\Theta=-f+2\bar K g+(1-2\lambda)\bar K\beta.
\end{equation}
Applying $E_\pm$ and \cref{eq:intertwine} produces \cref{eq:evenGreen}.  Direct
left and right multiplication uses $RC_\pm^2=C_\pm=C_\pm^2R$; the residual
off-diagonal coefficient is
$-\tfrac12+\tfrac{1-2\lambda}{2}+\lambda=0$.
Locality of $D,\bar K$ and transitivity of causal futures/pasts prove the support
estimate.  Equation \cref{eq:oddGreen} is the direct inverse of the off-diagonal
normally hyperbolic block.
\end{proof}

Let
\begin{equation}
 J_{\mathrm{nm}}=\operatorname{diag}
 \left(\cA,\kappa,\frac{\kappa}{2},-\frac{\kappa}{2}\right).
\end{equation}
The local benchmark Green maps are
\begin{equation}
 G_\pm^{(P_{\mathrm{nm},\lambda}^{\mathrm{b}})}
 =\frac{\kappa^2}{4}J_{\mathrm{nm}}^{-1}
 G_\pm^{(P_{\mathrm{nm},\lambda}^{H})}(J_{\mathrm{nm}}^\dagger)^{-1}.
 \label{eq:LBGreenpull}
\end{equation}
At $\lambda=1/2$ the $C_\pm^2$ term vanishes.  For $\lambda\neq0$ one may eliminate
$b$ and obtain the equation operator
\begin{equation}
 \bar P_\lambda=\bar P+\lambda^{-1}\bar K D,
 \qquad
 G_\pm^{(\bar P_\lambda)}=E_\pm+\frac{2\lambda-1}{2}\bar K C_\pm^2D.
\end{equation}
At $\lambda=0$ the Schur complement does not exist, although the full block Green
operator remains well defined.

\begin{remark}[Source boundary]
Normal hyperbolicity of $\widetilde P$ and $R$, together with their source convention,
is specialized from \cite{Hack2014}.  The four-by-four nonminimal matrix,
\cref{eq:evenGreen,eq:oddGreen}, its $\lambda=0$ treatment, and the exact local benchmark pullback
are the derived composition.  The result proves propagation only.  It does not produce
a state or a physical representation.
\end{remark}

\section{Reduced phase space and the Fuchsian Hadamard host}

\subsection{Gauge-invariant test quotient}

Use the source pairing
\begin{equation}
 \langle(\theta,\zeta),(\theta',\zeta')\rangle_F
 =\int_{\U}\left(4\theta^{\mu\nu}\theta'_{\mu\nu}
 -2\operatorname{tr}\theta\operatorname{tr}\theta'+\zeta\zeta'\right)
 \dd\mu_{\gbar}.
 \label{eq:sourcepair}
\end{equation}
The compact-test quotient is
\begin{equation}
 \cE^H_{\U}=\frac{\Ker(D:\Gamma_c(\cV)\to\Gamma_c(T\U))}
 {\bar P\Gamma_c(\cV)},
 \label{eq:testquotient}
\end{equation}
where $\cV=S^2T^*\U\oplus\mathbb R$.  At $\bar\phi=0$ it splits algebraically into
graviton and scalar summands.  If $G=G_+-G_-$ is the reduced causal propagator,
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_H([f],[g])=\langle f,Gg\rangle_F
 \label{eq:sigmaH}
\end{equation}
is a well-defined presymplectic form.  The local benchmark form is
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_{\mathrm{b}}(f,g)=\frac{\kappa^2}{4}
 \sigma_H(J^\vee f,J^\vee g),
 \qquad J^\vee=(J^*)^{-1}.
 \label{eq:sigmaLB}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the identified commutators use
$\hbar_H=(\kappa^2/4)\hbarLB$.

The quotient defines both a polynomial CCR algebra and, separately, the Weyl
$C^*$-algebra
\begin{equation}
 W(f)W(g)=e^{-\frac{i}{2}\hbarLB\sigma_{\mathrm{b}}(f,g)}W(f+g),
 \qquad W(f)^*=W(-f).
 \label{eq:weyl}
\end{equation}
Degeneracy of $\sigma$ produces central Weyl elements but does not obstruct the
universal construction.  Causal support yields Einstein commutativity for disjoint
compact representatives.  Standard Green-hyperbolic deformation gives a
fixed-ambient time-slice statement.  These are source-specialized consequences of
\cite{Hack2014,HackSchenkel2013,FewsterHunt2013}, not new general CCR theorems.

\subsection{A compact-Cauchy locally-AdS host}

The positive state is constructed on a refined domain.  A Fuchsian AdS region has
the form
\begin{equation}
 E_v\simeq(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\times\mathbb H^3,
 \qquad
 g_E=-L^2\dd\tau^2+\cos^2\tau\,h_{\mathbb H^3,L}.
 \label{eq:fuchsian}
\end{equation}
Choose $\U$ with compact closure $K\Subset E_v$.  Let $\Gamma_0$ be a torsion-free
cocompact lattice in $SO_0(1,3)$.  Only finitely many nonidentity translates
$\gamma K$ can be causally related to $K$.  Residual finiteness permits passage to a
finite-index normal subgroup $\Gamma$ excluding this finite bad set.  The quotient
\begin{equation}
 \M=\Gamma\backslash E_v
 \label{eq:host}
\end{equation}
is a four-dimensional globally hyperbolic Einstein spacetime with compact Cauchy
surface $\Gamma\backslash\mathbb H^3$, and the quotient map restricts to a causally
convex isometric embedding $i:\U\hookrightarrow\M$.  The geometric input belongs to
the Fuchsian AdS theory surveyed in \cite{Barbot2013}; the finite bad-set refinement is
the derived host-selection argument.

\subsection{Reduced positive Hadamard state}

G\'erard's theorem applies to $\M$ because it is four-dimensional, globally
hyperbolic, Einstein, and has compact Cauchy surfaces \cite{Gerard2023}.  Let
$\Lambda_G^\pm$ be the resulting positive gauge-invariant graviton Hadamard
covariances.  The source convention uses a constant trace-reversal bridge
$T_g=2I$.  Its physical pairing is a factor-two homothety, so define
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_{H,g}^\pm(q,r)=\frac12\Lambda_G^\pm(T_gq,T_gr).
 \label{eq:factorhalf}
\end{equation}
Choose scalar Klein--Gordon Hadamard covariances on the same host, whose existence is
standard \cite{GerardWrochna2014,Radzikowski1996}.  Since the free blocks decouple at
$\bar\phi=0$, take the direct sum and pull it back by zero extension:
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_{H,\U}^\pm(q,r)=\Lambda_{H,\M}^\pm(i_*q,i_*r).
 \label{eq:pullstate}
\end{equation}
Finally set
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_{\mathrm{b},\U}^\pm(f,g)=
 \hbar_H\Lambda_{H,\U}^\pm(J^\vee f,J^\vee g).
 \label{eq:LBcov}
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Imported physical Hadamard state]
Use the positive gauge-invariant graviton state on the compact-Cauchy Einstein
host and a scalar Hadamard state, with the normalization
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_{\mathrm{b},\U}^+-\Lambda_{\mathrm{b},\U}^-
=i\hbarLB\sigma_{\mathrm{b}}.
\label{eq:reducedcomm}
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}
Existence in the present decoupled Einstein-host setting follows from the cited
source results and the causal restriction map. A separate companion construction
proves the coupled canonical Einstein--scalar physical Hadamard theorem for a

potential-gradient condition along the on-shell background image
\cite{CanonicalPhysical2026}. That theorem is cited rather than re-proved here.
The formulas above record the normalization required to use its compatible
reduced state in this benchmark; no claim of a second new state-existence theorem
is made. In particular, a physical-state theorem alone does not provide the

full nonminimal BRST covariance requires more than this theorem; that construction is
the subject of the next section.

\section{Conditional Ward completion and nonminimal transport}
The following composition uses the declared compact-host Fredholm and Hadamard
inputs. Its new content is the explicit left-inverse/Ward-repair and convention
transport algebra. The general microlocal and compact-test quotient machinery is
imported from the cited results, including the companion physical-state theorem;
finite coefficient checks are not proofs of those analytic inputs.


\subsection{Cauchy complex and a tame left inverse}

Let $\Sigma_\Gamma$ be a compact Cauchy surface of $\M$.  In G\'erard's adapted
Sobolev scale write
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf H_j^s=H^s(\Sigma_\Gamma;V_j)\oplus
 H^{s-1}(\Sigma_\Gamma;V_j),
\end{equation}
and denote the Cauchy gauge map by
\begin{equation}
 k=K_\Sigma:\mathsf H_1^{s+1}\longrightarrow\mathsf H_2^s.
\end{equation}
G\'erard's gauge-fixing map gives an operator $R_\Sigma$ such that
$A=R_\Sigma k$ is Fredholm of index zero and has an elliptic first-order diagonal
part plus a smoothing remainder \cite{Gerard2023}.  Choose a pseudodifferential
generalized inverse $G$ with
\begin{equation}
 GA=1-\pi_F,
 \qquad F=\Ker A.
\end{equation}

For the chosen Fuchsian quotient, $\Ker k=0$.  A global Killing one-form lifts to a
$\Gamma$-invariant element of $\mathfrak{so}(2,3)$.  Borel density reduces invariance
to the centralizer of the embedded $\mathfrak{so}(1,3)$, and that centralizer is zero.
Let $E=kF$, let $k_F:F\to E$ be the resulting isomorphism, and choose a smoothing
projection $\pi_E$ onto $E$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 L=GR_\Sigma+k_F^{-1}\pi_E(1-kGR_\Sigma).
 \label{eq:leftinverse}
\end{equation}
The inverse $k_F^{-1}$ is applied only after $\pi_E$.

\begin{proposition}[Fredholm left inverse; derived]
The operator \cref{eq:leftinverse} obeys
\begin{equation}
 Lk=1,
 \qquad Q_B=kL,
 \qquad Q_B^2=Q_B,
 \qquad \Ran Q_B=\Ran k=:B.
 \label{eq:projectB}
\end{equation}
It is of order $-1$ in the adapted scale up to a finite-rank smoothing term.  Hence
$B$ is closed and complemented at every Sobolev order, with a compatible smooth
Fr\'echet splitting.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $x\in\mathsf H_1^{s+1}$,
$GR_\Sigma kx=(1-\pi_F)x$, and
$(1-kGR_\Sigma)kx=k\pi_Fx\in E$.  Substitution into
\cref{eq:leftinverse} gives $Lkx=x$.  The remaining identities follow immediately.
Pseudodifferential order and finite-rank smoothness follow from the selected parametrix
and projections.  The estimates are inherited uniformly through the compatible
Sobolev scale.
\end{proof}

Let $q_j$ denote the nondegenerate Cauchy charges, interpreted as dualities
$\mathsf H_j^s\times\mathsf H_j^{1-s}\to\mathbb C$.  Put
\begin{equation}
 C=\Ker k^\dagger,
 \qquad B=\Ran k\subset C.
\end{equation}
The charge adjoint of $Lk=1$ gives $k^\dagger L^\dagger=1$.  Consequently
\begin{equation}
 Q_C=1-L^\dagger k^\dagger
\end{equation}
projects onto $C$, and its dual-scale adjoint has kernel $B$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 C^{\perp q_2}=B.
 \label{eq:coisotropic}
\end{equation}
The use of dual Sobolev orders is essential; no same-order adjoint is silently assumed.

\subsection{Retaining the selected physical state}

G\'erard's regular and singular constructions provide a quotient selector $p$ on $C$
such that
\begin{equation}
 pc=c\mod B,
 \qquad pk=0,
 \qquad C=B\oplus P,
 \qquad P=\Ran(p|_C).
 \label{eq:pselector}
\end{equation}
In the singular branch $p$ contains a finite-rank correction and therefore retains,
rather than discards, the finite-dimensional singular physical sector.

Let $\mu_2^\pm$ and $\mu_1^\pm$ be the unmodified tensor and vector Hadamard
pseudocovariances.  The desired reduced tensor state and the restriction of $\mu_2$
to $C/B$ have the same causal difference.  Their common difference $d_C$ is smoothing.
Extend it to all tensor Cauchy data by
\begin{equation}
 a_d(x,y)=d_C(Q_Cx,Q_Cy).
 \label{eq:dextension}
\end{equation}
Adding the evolved bisolution associated with $a_d$ equally to the two tensor signs
preserves the causal difference and Hadamard wavefront set while making the restriction
to $C\times C$ exactly the selected reduced state.

The tensor/vector Ward relation may initially hold only modulo a common smoothing
defect
\begin{equation}
 S=k\widehat\lambda_1^\pm-\widehat\lambda_2^{\prime\pm}k.
\end{equation}
For every $c\in C$ and $x$ in the vector Cauchy space, $q_2(c,Sx)=0$.  By
\cref{eq:coisotropic}, $Sx\in B$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 F=SL,
 \qquad A_2=F+F^\dagger,
 \qquad A_1=0.
 \label{eq:wardcorrection}
\end{equation}
Because $B$ is isotropic, $F^\dagger b=0$ for $b\in B$, and
\begin{equation}
 A_2k=S,
 \qquad q_2(c,A_2d)=0\quad(c,d\in C).
\end{equation}
Thus the corrected tensor covariances
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\lambda_2^\pm:=\widehat\lambda_2^{\prime\pm}+A_2
 \qquad\text{satisfy}\qquad
 k\widehat\lambda_1^\pm-\widehat\lambda_2^\pm k=0.
\end{equation}
The common smooth correction is invisible to the physical state and repairs the Ward
identity exactly.  This direct formula avoids an unproved global Witt normal
form.  It is a project-level composition of the Fredholm data in \cite{Gerard2023}
and the abstract subsidiary/BRST mechanism in
\cite{GerardWrochna2017,WrochnaZahn2015}.

\subsection{Pairing-preserving transport to the five-field matrix}

In grouped orders

 \text{WZ}: (u,b_W,c,\cbar),
 \qquad
 \text{source}: (\Theta,\beta,\eta,\bar\eta),
\end{equation}
define configuration and Euler-row maps

 C_0=\operatorname{diag}(T_g,-1,1,-1),
 \qquad
 R_0=\operatorname{diag}(-\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12).
 \label{eq:CRmaps}
\end{equation}
With the declared graded forms $B_{\mathrm{WZ}}$ and $B_Q$, one has

 P_Q=R_0L_{\mathrm{WZ}}C_0,

 \label{eq:pairduality}
\end{equation}
The second identity is the missing pairing statement: $R_0$ is not simply

 \lambda_Q^\pm=C_0^{-1}\lambda_{\mathrm{WZ}}^\pm R_0^{-1}.
 \label{eq:covtransport}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 P_Q\lambda_Q^\pm=\lambda_Q^\pm P_Q=0,
 \qquad
 \lambda_Q^+-(-1)^{\gh}\lambda_Q^-=iG_Q,
 \label{eq:q2ids}
\end{equation}
and the associated forms are Hermitian and positive on the transported physical

The configuration codifferential and its pairing adjoint are

 \gamma_Q(\Theta,\beta,\eta,\bar\eta)
 =(\bar K\eta,0,0,\beta),
 \qquad
 \gamma_Q^\star(\Theta,\beta,\eta,\bar\eta)
 =(0,\bar\eta,-D\Theta,0).
 \label{eq:gammaQ}
\end{equation}
Their exact Ward compatibility follows from the repaired tensor/vector pair.  Add the

 \lambda_{\mathrm{b},1}^\pm
 =c_0J_{\mathrm{nm}}^{-1}\lambda_H^\pm J_{\mathrm{nm}}^\vee,

 \label{eq:unittransport}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the dimensionful form is

 \Lambda_{\mathrm{b}}^\pm(f,g)=

 \label{eq:dimtransport}
\end{equation}
One uses either \cref{eq:unittransport} followed by $\hbarLB$ or

\begin{theorem}[Full free-local BRST Hadamard system]
At $\lambda=1/2$, the host construction and

\section{Equicausal free field algebra and time slice}

\subsection{Why the unrestricted microcausal class is not used}

For $n\geq1$, let
\begin{equation}
 \Xi_n=(T^*\U^n\setminus0)\setminus
 (\overline V_+^n\cup\overline V_-^n).
\end{equation}
The usual microcausal condition asks
$\WF(F^{(n)}(\Phi))\subset\Xi_n$ at each configuration.  This pointwise condition is
insufficient: a Peierls bracket of regular functionals can fail to be smooth, so the
unrestricted class is not closed \cite{HawkinsRejznerVisser2023}.

We instead use the graded equicausal space $\cF_{\mathrm{ec}}^{\mathrm{gr}}$.  Its
elements have finite algebraic ghost and antighost degrees, are Bastiani smooth in
$(h,\phi,b)$, have compactly supported mixed derivatives, obey the same wavefront
exclusion, and satisfy the equicontinuity condition of
\cite{HawkinsRejznerVisser2023} uniformly on compact subsets of the even
configuration space.  At fixed contraction order, the five internal species produce
only a finite sum of ordinary bundle-component contractions.  Koszul permutations
alter signs but not wavefront sets.  The source estimates therefore extend
componentwise to the finite graded multiplet.  This is a source-specialized analytic
step, not a claim about a completed exterior topology.

\subsection{Contraction kernel and star product}

Let $W^+$ and $W^-$ be the form-level members of the full Hadamard pair, including the
Schur sign in the minus member.  To avoid silently assuming a crossing identity not
contained in the state theorem, define
\begin{equation}
 W=\frac12\left(W^++\tau_{\mathrm{gr}}W^-\right),
 \label{eq:Wsym}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{\mathrm{gr}}$ exchanges arguments and inserts the graded species sign.
Ordinary Hermitian conjugation is complex conjugation followed by ungraded argument
exchange; reversal of the exterior coefficient product already supplies the odd
signs.  Then
\begin{equation}
 W^\dagger=W,
 \qquad W-\tau_{\mathrm{gr}}W=iE_G.
 \label{eq:Wproperties}
\end{equation}
The symmetrized kernel is a Hadamard contraction with the correct commutator.  It is
not asserted to be the selected positive Hadamard covariance.

For homogeneous equicausal functionals define
\begin{equation}
 F\star_WG
 =m_{\mathrm{gr}}\exp(\hbarLB\Gamma_W)(F\otimes G)
 =\sum_{n\geq0}\frac{\hbarLB^n}{n!}
 \left\langle F_l^{(n)},W^{\otimes n}G_r^{(n)}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{gr}}.
 \label{eq:star}
\end{equation}
Every coefficient is equicausal, and the product is associative
\cite{HawkinsRejznerVisser2023}.  Equations \cref{eq:Wproperties,eq:star} give
\begin{equation}
 (F\star_WG)^\dagger=G^\dagger\star_WF^\dagger,
 \qquad
 [F,G]_{\star,\mathrm{gr}}=i\hbarLB\{F,G\}_0+O(\hbarLB^2).
 \label{eq:starprops}
\end{equation}
For linear functionals the commutator formula is exact.

\subsection{Exact Ward identity and BRST descent}

Let $\Pi$ act as $+1$ on even and $-1$ on odd species.  In grouped source order, the
Hadamard covariances satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_Q\lambda_Q^+=\lambda_Q^+\gamma_Q^\star=:M,
 \qquad \Pi M=-M\Pi.
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \Pi\gamma_Q\lambda_Q^++\lambda_Q^+\gamma_Q^\star\Pi=0.
 \label{eq:ward2arg}
\end{equation}
The signed minus member and the symmetrization \cref{eq:Wsym} obey the same
two-argument identity.  This is precisely the covariance condition needed for the
unconjugated free BRST differential to be a star derivation
\cite{Rejzner2013}.

Let $s_0$ act as in \cref{eq:s0}.  It preserves the finite graded equicausal domain and
\begin{equation}
 s_0(F\star_WG)=(s_0F)\star_WG+(-1)^{|F|}F\star_W(s_0G).
 \label{eq:brstderivation}
\end{equation}
Let $\delta_P$ be the Koszul differential given by contraction with the linear equation
section $P_{\mathrm{nm},1/2}^{\mathrm{b}}\Phi$.  The exact covariance relation implies
\begin{equation}
 [s_0,\delta_P]_{\mathrm{gr}}=0.
\end{equation}
The on-shell algebra is the degree-zero Koszul homology
\begin{equation}
 \cA_{\mathrm{ec,on}}(\U)=
 H_0(\mathcal X_{\mathrm{ec}}^{\mathrm{gr},*}[[\hbarLB]],\delta_P),
 \label{eq:onshellalg}
\end{equation}
not merely a quotient by the algebraic ideal generated by constant linear equations.
The full Koszul image is a two-sided star ideal.  The free physical product algebra is
\begin{equation}
 \cA_{\mathrm{phys,free}}=
 H^0(\cA_{\mathrm{ec,on}}(\U),s_0).
 \label{eq:physicalalg}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Einstein causality and fixed-ambient time slice}

If homogeneous $F,G$ have causally disjoint functional supports, every cross
contraction with the causal kernel vanishes.  Equation \cref{eq:Wproperties} then
implies, term by term,
\begin{equation}
 F\star_WG=(-1)^{|F||G|}G\star_WF.
 \label{eq:einsteincausality}
\end{equation}

Let $N\subset\U$ be open and contain a Cauchy surface of $\U$.  The Green-hyperbolic time-slice
projection and its chain homotopy preserve equicausality
\cite{HawkinsRejznerVisser2023}.  Inclusion of the fixed-ambient support subcomplex
inside $N$ is therefore a quasi-isomorphism and induces
\begin{equation}
 \cA_{\mathrm{ec,on}}(N)\simeq\cA_{\mathrm{ec,on}}(\U),
 \qquad
 H^0(\cA_{\mathrm{ec,on}}(N),s_0)
 \simeq H^0(\cA_{\mathrm{ec,on}}(\U),s_0)
 \label{eq:timeslice}
\end{equation}
as star and product algebras, respectively.  The first is compatible with the ordinary
dagger before BRST cohomology.  The second is deliberately stated only as a product
isomorphism; the dagger obstruction below prevents a stronger claim in the frozen
convention.

Renormalized causal factorization is more restricted.  Epstein--Glaser maps exist for
compactly supported local, fiberwise analytic inputs and their finite multilocal
products \cite{BrunettiFredenhagen2000,BrunettiFredenhagenRejzner2016,
GwilliamRejzner2022}.  For causally ordered supports they obey
\begin{equation}
 T_n(F_1,\ldots,F_n)=T_k(F_1,\ldots,F_k)\star_W
 T_{n-k}(F_{k+1},\ldots,F_n).
 \label{eq:causalfactor}
\end{equation}
This source-specialized local/multilocal row does not extend $T_n$ to every equicausal
functional.  Nor does it by itself descend through the equation ideal or BRST
cohomology; collision terms and local anomalies remain.

\section{Physical reduction, local injectivity, and the dagger obstruction}
\label{sec:physicalreduction}

\subsection{Nonminimal contraction}

Before gauge fixing, the nonminimal jets obey
\begin{equation}
 s_0\cbar=b,
 \quad s_0b=0,
 \quad s_0b^\ddagger=-\cbar^\ddagger,
 \quad s_0\cbar^\ddagger=0.
\end{equation}
Define the odd right derivation $r$ by
\begin{equation}
 r(D_Ib)=D_I\cbar,
 \qquad r(D_I\cbar^\ddagger)=-D_Ib^\ddagger,
 \qquad r(D_I\cbar)=r(D_Ib^\ddagger)=0.
\end{equation}
If $N_{\mathrm{nm}}$ counts nonminimal jets, then
\begin{equation}
 s_0r+rs_0=N_{\mathrm{nm}}.
\end{equation}
Division by positive homogeneous degree gives a support-preserving contracting homotopy.
For finite-jet fiberwise analytic germs the equivalent scaling integral is
\begin{equation}
 H_{\mathrm{nm}}=r\int_0^1\frac{\dd t}{t}
 (\sigma_t-\iota\pi),
 \qquad
 s_0H_{\mathrm{nm}}+H_{\mathrm{nm}}s_0=1-\iota\pi.
 \label{eq:nmhomotopy}
\end{equation}
Conjugating \cref{eq:nmhomotopy} by the canonical gauge-fixing transformation gives
the corresponding gauge-fixed contraction.  The transported minimal projection is
not naive deletion of $\cbar,b$ and their antifields.

At ghost number zero, compact linear BV cohomology is consequently the reduced
test-space phase \cref{eq:testquotient}.  This exact local contraction is a project
specialization of the standard nonminimal-doublet mechanism
\cite{BarnichBrandtHenneaux2000}.

\subsection{No extra null vectors on the host}

The physical gravitational Cauchy space is
\begin{equation}
 V_g=C/B,
 \qquad q_g([c],[d])=q_2(c,d).
\end{equation}
Equation \cref{eq:coisotropic} makes $q_g$ weakly nondegenerate.  Add the ordinary
nondegenerate scalar phase space and write $V_{\M}=V_g\oplus V_\phi$.

Let $\Lambda_{\M}^\pm$ be the induced positive physical two-point forms.  Their sum
\begin{equation}
 N_{\M}=\Lambda_{\M}^++\Lambda_{\M}^-
\end{equation}
is positive definite.  If $N_{\M}(v,v)=0$, positivity and Cauchy--Schwarz make both
$\Lambda_{\M}^\pm(w,v)$ vanish for all $w$.  Their causal difference then gives
$q_{\M}(w,v)=0$ for all $w$, and nondegeneracy implies $v=0$.  Wrochna--Zahn's
criterion therefore yields a ghost-number-zero pre-Hilbert space with no BRST-closed,
nonexact zero-norm vector on the host \cite{WrochnaZahn2015}.

\subsection{Injectivity of local zero extension}

The local state is a pullback along the physical map
\begin{equation}
 I_{\mathrm{phys}}:V_{\U}\longrightarrow V_{\M},
 \qquad [f]\longmapsto[i_*f].
 \label{eq:Iphys}
\end{equation}
Its covariance radical is exactly $\Ker I_{\mathrm{phys}}$.  Thus host positivity does
not by itself prove local faithfulness.

Choose $\U$ to be a standard contractible double cone, diffeomorphic to $\mathbb R^4$.
For the Calabi complex on a constant-curvature spacetime, causally restricted
cohomology gives
\begin{equation}
 H^1_{\mathrm{Cal},\mathrm{sc}}(\U)
 \simeq H^2_{\mathrm{Cal},c}(\U)=0
 \label{eq:calabivanishing}
\end{equation}
under the stated topology \cite{Khavkine2014}.  Hence every spacelike-compact
pure-gauge metric perturbation admits a spacelike-compact gauge parameter on $\U$.

Suppose $[f]\in V_{\U}$ maps to zero.  The host test/solution quotient theorem
\cite{HackSchenkel2013} gives a spacelike-compact $\epsilon_{\M}$ with
\begin{equation}
 G_{\M} i_*f=K_{\M}\epsilon_{\M}.
\end{equation}
Restriction of Green operators to a causally compatible region
\cite{Baer2015} gives
\begin{equation}
 G_{\U} f=K_{\U}(i^*\epsilon_{\M}).
\end{equation}
The left side is spacelike compact even if $i^*\epsilon_{\M}$ need not be.  By
\cref{eq:calabivanishing} it equals $K_{\U}\epsilon_{\U}$ for a spacelike-compact local
parameter.  Injectivity of the local test/solution quotient then implies $[f]=0$.
Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \Ker I_{\mathrm{phys}}=0.
 \label{eq:Iinjective}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Local no-extra-null physical space]
On the selected standard double cone $\U$, the ghost-number-zero free BRST physical
form is positive definite at fixed $\hbarLB>0$.  A BRST-closed one-particle vector has
zero norm if and only if it is BRST exact.
\end{theorem}

This conclusion is narrower than Peierls nondegeneracy.  A distinct adjoint Calabi
parametrizability problem retains local Killing data, so the state-independent
presymplectic radical and an independently functorial net are not promoted here.

\subsection{The ordinary dagger does not descend}

The ordinary dagger is valid on the off-shell and on-shell star algebra.  It fails on
the same $s_0$ cohomology.  Define the adjoint differential on a homogeneous functional
by
\begin{equation}
 s_\sharp F=(-1)^{|F|}\bigl(s_0(F^\dagger)\bigr)^\dagger.
\end{equation}
The minimal and nonminimal pieces satisfy
\begin{equation}
 s_\sharp=s_{\min}-s_{\mathrm{nm}},
\end{equation}
so dagger is an anti-isomorphism between the $s_0$ and $s_\sharp$ complexes, not an
involution of the $s_0$ complex.

For an explicit counterexample choose real linear smearings for which the on-shell
classes $C=s_0H$ and $B=s_0\ell_{\cbar}$ are nonzero and satisfy
$C\star B\neq0$.  Such choices exist in the nontrivial free ghost--auxiliary
polynomial sector; this nonvanishing condition is essential and is not asserted for
arbitrary smearings.  Abbreviate
\begin{equation}
 H=\ell_h,
 \qquad C=s_0H,
 \qquad B=s_0\ell_{\cbar}.
\end{equation}
The ghost-number-minus-one element $A=\ell_{\cbar}\star H$ has boundary
\begin{equation}
 F=s_0A=B\star H-\ell_{\cbar}\star C.
\end{equation}
Ordinary reversal gives $F^\dagger=H\star B-C\star\ell_{\cbar}$ and hence
\begin{equation}
 s_0(F^\dagger)=2C\star B\neq0.
 \label{eq:daggercounter}
\end{equation}
Thus dagger sends an explicit boundary outside the cycle space.

\begin{theorem}[Dagger-descent obstruction; derived]
In the frozen nonminimal reality convention, the ordinary star-algebra dagger does not
descend to $H^0(\cA_{\mathrm{ec,on}},s_0)$.
\end{theorem}

One may seek an adjoint-BRST double-kernel construction or transfer a dagger from a
reduced physical algebra after nonminimal contraction.  Neither construction is
provided here.  Accordingly the physical object in \cref{eq:physicalalg} is presently
a product algebra, while the no-extra-null theorem is a one-particle/pre-Hilbert
statement.

\section{Matter deformation and the quantum stop line}

\subsection{The exact \texorpdfstring{$h\phi\phi$}{h-phi-phi} vertex}

Expanding the scalar action using
\begin{equation}
 g^{\mu\nu}=\gbar^{\mu\nu}-\kappa h^{\mu\nu}+O(\kappa^2),
 \qquad
 \dd\mu_g=(1+\tfrac\kappa2h)\dd\mu_{\gbar}+O(\kappa^2),
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{equation}
 S_m[\gbar+\kappa h,\phi]
 =S_{\phi,0}+\kappa V_{h\phi\phi}+O(\kappa^2),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
 V_{h\phi\phi}
 &=\int_{\U}\left[
 \frac12h^{\mu\nu}\bar\nabla_\mu\phi\bar\nabla_\nu\phi
 -\frac14h\bigl((\bar\nabla\phi)^2+m^2\phi^2\bigr)
 \right]\dd\mu_{\gbar}\notag\\
 &=\frac12\int_{\U}h_{\mu\nu}T_0^{\mu\nu}[\phi]\dd\mu_{\gbar}.
 \label{eq:hphiphi}
\end{align}
Direct differentiation yields the off-shell identity
\begin{equation}
 \bar\nabla^\mu T^0_{\mu\nu}=(P_\phi\phi)\bar\nabla_\nu\phi.
 \label{eq:stressdiv}
\end{equation}
This is exactly the compatibility needed by the linearized Bianchi identity on the free
scalar shell.

\subsection{Scalar-antifield completion}

The first nonlinear scalar transformation is $s_1\phi=\mathcal L_c\phi$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 A_\phi=\int_{\U}\phi^\ddagger c^\mu\bar\nabla_\mu\phi,
 \qquad
 S_{1,m}=V_{h\phi\phi}+A_\phi.
 \label{eq:S1m}
\end{equation}
The antifield is density valued, so no second volume density appears.  With the frozen
left/right derivative convention,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\delta_lA_\phi}{\delta\phi^\ddagger}=+\mathcal L_c\phi,
 \qquad
 \frac{\delta_rA_\phi}{\delta\phi^\ddagger}=-\mathcal L_c\phi.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What is genuinely learned}

The central lesson is that a plausible verbal stack---``BV plus Hadamard plus BRST
plus pAQFT''---is not a theorem.  Compatibility requires a series of maps.  The
source-to-benchmark action normalization fixes the sign of the Euler operator.  The
G\'erard-to-source bridge contributes a single factor one half.  The Wrochna--Zahn
configuration and Euler-row maps are dual only after the pairing identity
\cref{eq:pairduality}.  The nonminimal field matrix is Green hyperbolic even when the
Schur complement is undefined.  Host positivity reaches the local algebra only after
zero-extension injectivity.  Finally, the same ghost convention that makes the
covariance and BRST Ward identity work can obstruct descent of the ordinary dagger.

These are modest compared with constructing quantum gravity, but they are not empty
bookkeeping.  They identify where familiar theorems genuinely compose and where an
additional physical or analytic input is needed.

\subsection{Why the dagger failure is useful}

The explicit counterexample \cref{eq:daggercounter} prevents a common silent move:
declaring that because the off-shell star algebra and BRST complex each have reasonable
reality properties, the cohomology automatically inherits the same adjoint.  It does
not.  A transferred adjoint may still exist, but it must be constructed together with
the nonminimal reduction or a physical completion.  For boundary reconstruction this
matters because positivity and operational interpretation require a specified
involution, not merely a complex product algebra.

\subsection{Interactions are the decisive next test}

The classical matter subcocycle is a useful first deformation because its cancellation
directly exposes the stress identity and the antifield sign.  Yet it is only one
summand.  The next scientifically meaningful step is not another general criterion.
It is to assemble the full order-$\kappa$ BV deformation in one convention, construct
renormalized time-ordered maps on its declared domain, and compute the first local
anomaly class.  Only after that calculation can the free benchmark be promoted to an
interacting local sector.

\section{Conclusion}

We have assembled a self-contained local Einstein--scalar BV/BRST benchmark on a
globally hyperbolic interior region of AdS$_4$.  The construction reaches farther than
a formal classical complex: it includes the full nonminimal Green matrix, a selected

deformation.  It also records one exact obstruction---the ordinary dagger does not

assumed.  It does not derive that dynamics, construct global AdS quantum gravity, or

\section{Direct block verification of the Green matrix}

For convenience suppress the advanced/retarded sign and set
\begin{equation}

\begin{equation}
 B_\lambda
 \begin{pmatrix}X&Y\\Z&0\end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -\bar PX+\bar KZ&-\bar PY\\
 DX+\lambda Z&DY
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Using $\bar PE=1-\bar KCD$, $\bar P\bar K=0$, $D\bar K=R$, $RC=1$ on compact

 -\frac12CD+\alpha CD+\lambda CD

\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
 \begin{pmatrix}0&-R\\R&0\end{pmatrix}

\end{equation}
with the same result in the reversed order.  This algebraic verification explains why
the full matrix exists at $\lambda=0$ although a Schur formula does not.
