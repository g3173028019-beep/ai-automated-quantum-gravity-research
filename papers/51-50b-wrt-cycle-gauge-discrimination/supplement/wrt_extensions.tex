\begin{abstract}
We give four concrete extensions of the paired-cap sewing calculations.
First, for every perfectly matched tree, actual fixed-spin Wilson
insertions with selected physical color-ratio marks admit a resolved
angular law without matched-product cutoffs. The resulting measure
imposes shifted perpendicularity constraints and has a positive
integrable density. On the four-site path this gives an explicit nonzero
$r^{5/2}\log r$ coefficient, including an exact hard-window constant.
Second, every fixed even cycle has leading term
$\binom{2m-2}{m-1}\pi^{-2m}r^{2m}\log r$; the proof is an exact
positive reduction followed by a convolution trace calculation.
Third, angular filters of the four-site Hall normal phase successively
remove its two logarithms; a Fourier--Gaussian estimate controls the
singular angular intersections. Finally, an integral homology formula
strengthens the signed-sewing collision mechanism. An explicit pair
is indistinguishable by every SU(2) level, every odd pointed theory,
and every twisted finite odd nilpotent gauge theory, while its
two-primary homology differs. All statements include proofs and
specified dependencies. The fully ungated subleading finite-level
Wilson coefficient remains an explicit open estimate.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Status and conventions}
This is a working research note with complete derivations of the
statements made below, prepared in response to a request to extend
Papers 51 and 50B. It is not a report of external peer review or a
priority claim. The source files and exact verification scripts are
included in the accompanying bundle.

The quantum Wilson theorem uses three specific statements of
Paper 51~\cite{p51}: the finite-rank tree reduction, the joint
compact-product symbol theorem, and the signed whole-alcove prefix
bound. Its proof is an extension from those inputs, rather than a
new independent construction of the entire theory. The cycle theorem
uses the finite sewing formula and degree-two identity of Paper
50~\cite{p50}. The topological statements are proved directly from
the filled-block relations; their oriented RT normalization uses
the Maslov computation of Paper 50B~\cite{p50b}.

Throughout, $r=k+2$ is the shifted SU(2) level,
$Z_r(S^2\times S^1)=1$, $Z_r(S^3)=S_{11}$, and
$\tau_r=Z_r/S_{11}$. Graph sizes and inserted spins are fixed as
$r\to\infty$. The physical Chern--Simons phase is $e^{2\pi i r CS}$.
``Next logarithm'' refers to the next power of $\log r$ at a
specified power of $r$, not to a universal enumeration of all strata.

\paragraph{How to read the results.}
Section~\ref{sec:ratio} is an actual finite-level quantum asymptotic
with declared ratio and ordinary radial marks.
Section~\ref{sec:cycles} is a closed, uninserted quantum asymptotic.
Section~\ref{sec:p4-next-log-model} concerns a specified observable
in the exact classical normal phase; its final subsection records
what is still needed to transfer the proposed ungated coefficient
to the quantum invariant.
The remaining sections give exact integral and representation-theoretic
statements, independent of semiclassical limits.

\section{Verification and source versions}
The finite checks are independent diagnostics for the constants and
integral presentations, not replacements for the proofs above.
\begin{itemize}
\item The even-cycle script reconstructs the caps directly from the
SU(2) modular matrices and compares the resulting transfer trace to
the reduced minimum kernel at $24$ levels, for cycles of lengths
$4,6,8$: $72$ equalities, with maximum relative floating-point error
$1.31\times10^{-13}$. The additional fixed-size samples through
$r=1602$ are consistent with the proved $O(r^{2m})$ remainder.
\item The homology script uses exact integer Smith forms. For both
eight-vertex examples it compares the $24$-generator cap--fibre
presentation with $4A_\epsilon$, and verifies the cyclic gauge
counts, including $2^{58}$ and $2^{60}$ at group order $16$.
\item The Gaussian script evaluates the explicit two-dimensional
integral. At $R=10^6$, the subtracted constant is
$29.539090460343$, compared to the analytic value
$29.539090469029$. This check concerns the normal model only.
\end{itemize}
