\begin{abstract}
A common radial source, finite autonomous evolution and a rank-one radial herald define a controlled-phase gate in a specified independent-field Dirac--Born--Infeld (DBI) half-line model.
At the frozen dimensionless time $107.61733846478977$, a diagonal successful Kraus operator lies within $0.18972055982017294$ of a unitary locally equivalent to controlled-$Z$.
Retained full-half-line residual certificates give success probability at least $0.6565527711781338$ for every logical input and conditional squared fidelity at least $0.96400610918152$, including arbitrary reference systems.
A stated source, readout, timing and local-phase error box retains success at least $0.6351321846638459$ and conditional squared fidelity at least $0.9587714318247369$.
The calculation uses a complete success/failure instrument and an elementary sharp norm-to-conditional-fidelity inequality.
The protocol assumes exact code preservation and access to the specified radial preparation, herald and local phase gates; their apparatus costs are not certified.
\end{abstract}

\section{The fixed model and the gate construction}
We adopt the dimensionless independent-field DBI radial model and invariant four-state logical code of the retained continuum calculation~\cite{continuumproof,trialdata}.
Its matter prescription is a sum of independent field Lagrangians, $\sum_jP(X_j)$; it is not the cross-coupled prescription $P(\sum_jX_j)$.
The model describes specified global zero-mode sectors, not spatially separated laboratory systems.
The present result is a new return-herald protocol within this fixed quantization, rather than a claim that this quantization is uniquely implied by a gravitational action.

Let $\mathcal H_r=L^2((0,\infty),dy)$ and $s=y^2$.
On the self-adjoint half-line realization inherited from the reference oscillator, define
\begin{align}
 A_{20}&=-\partial_y^2+y^2+20y^{-2},\label{eq:osc}\\
 A_b&=A_{20}-F_b(y^2),\qquad
 F_b(s)=\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_b}\bigl(\sqrt{s^2+2q}-s\bigr),\label{eq:model}\\
 q_{\rm bg}&=\frac{121}{729},\quad q_{\rm lo}=\frac{121}{1458},\quad q_{\rm hi}=\frac{4741}{1458},\nonumber\\
 \mathcal Q_{00}&=(q_{\rm bg},q_{\rm lo},q_{\rm lo}),\quad
 \mathcal Q_{01}=\mathcal Q_{10}=(q_{\rm bg},q_{\rm lo},q_{\rm hi}),\nonumber\\
 \mathcal Q_{11}&=(q_{\rm bg},q_{\rm hi},q_{\rm hi}).\nonumber
\end{align}
All fields are at the fixed finite nonlinear parameter $\theta=1$.
For $s\geq0$, $0\leq F_b(s)\leq\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_b}\sqrt{2q}$, so $F_b$ is bounded and $D(A_b)=D(A_{20})$.
Our dust-time convention is explicitly $U_b(\tau)=e^{+iA_b\tau}$.
Changing this sign would change the frozen phases; it is not a free convention within the certificate.

The normalized common radial source is
\begin{equation}
 \psi_5(y)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\Gamma(11/2)}}\,y^5e^{-y^2/2},
 \qquad A_{20}\psi_5=11\psi_5.\label{eq:source}
\end{equation}
Prepare it independently of an arbitrary two-qubit logical state, possibly entangled with an arbitrary reference.
Evolve with $\sum_b\ket b\bra b\otimes U_b(\tau)$, then measure the radial outcomes
\begin{equation}
 P=\ket{\psi_5}\bra{\psi_5},\qquad Q=I-P,
\end{equation}
keeping a flag for both outcomes.
The successful outcome resets the radial state exactly and induces the contraction
\begin{equation}
 K=\operatorname{diag}(k_{00},k_{01},k_{01},k_{11}),\qquad
 k_b=\bra{\psi_5}U_b(\tau)\ket{\psi_5}.\label{eq:K}
\end{equation}
The time and target phases are frozen decimal numbers,
\begin{align}
 \tau_*&=107.61733846478977,\label{eq:time}\\
 \phi_0&=0.9177158589349711,\qquad \phi_1=0.69393706341036732,\nonumber
\end{align}
and the target is the exactly unitary matrix
\begin{equation}
 D=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(e^{i\phi_0},e^{i\phi_1},e^{i\phi_1},
                         -e^{i(2\phi_1-\phi_0)}\bigr).\label{eq:D}
\end{equation}
Let $\delta=\phi_1-\phi_0=-0.22377879552460378$.
Apply $C=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{-i\delta})$ separately to each qubit on success.
Then
\begin{equation}
 (C\otimes C)D=e^{i\phi_0}\CZ.\label{eq:phase}
\end{equation}
In the convention $R_z(\alpha)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-i\alpha/2},e^{i\alpha/2})$, the corrective angle is $-\delta$, up to global phase.
Equation~\eqref{eq:phase} is algebraic: locating a transcendental root exactly is unnecessary.
The time was selected using finite-trial diagnostic return phases, then certified at that exact decimal time.
No global optimality of this time is claimed.

\section{Full-half-line residual control at the new time}
The supplied trial data~\cite{trialdata} are exact archived binary64 coefficients, not exact continuous eigenpairs.
The normalized basis is $\varphi_n=\psi_5p_n(s)$, with
\begin{equation}
 d\mu(s)=\frac{s^{9/2}e^{-s}}{\Gamma(11/2)}\,ds,\qquad
 A_{20}\varphi_n=(4n+11)\varphi_n.
\end{equation}
The polynomials are orthonormal for $d\mu$ and obey $p_0=1$ and
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt{(n+1)(n+11/2)}p_{n+1}
 =(s-2n-11/2)p_n-\sqrt{n(n+9/2)}p_{n-1},\label{eq:recurrence}
\end{equation}
where the last term is absent at $n=0$.
For each branch take eight trial functions in the first sixteen basis vectors:
\begin{equation}
 f_{bj}=\sum_{n=0}^{15}v_{bnj}\varphi_n,\qquad
 \widehat\psi_b(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{7}c_{bj}e^{i\lambda_{bj}t}f_{bj}.\label{eq:trial}
\end{equation}
All $v,c,\lambda$ are in the supplied JSON; exact orthogonality of the trial eigenvectors is unnecessary.
For $p_{bj}=\sum_nv_{bnj}p_n$ and $h_{bj}=\sum_n(4n+11-\lambda_{bj})v_{bnj}p_n$, the true residual norm is
\begin{equation}
 r_{bj}^2=\norm{(A_b-\lambda_{bj})f_{bj}}^2
 =\int_0^\infty|h_{bj}(s)-F_b(s)p_{bj}(s)|^2d\mu(s).\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
The retained certificates~\cite{residualdata} bound this entire integral; the half-line operator is not replaced by a spectral cutoff.

Their enclosure algorithm uses interval Taylor bounds on cells in $[0,128]$.
Writing $r(s)=h(s)-F(s)p(s)$, a cell of midpoint $m$ and half-width $h_c$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{\rm cell}|r|\leq |r(m)|+h_c|r'(m)|+
                \tfrac12h_c^2\sup_{\rm cell}|r''|.\label{eq:cell}
\end{equation}
The square times the cell width and the maximum gamma density bounds its integral.
The density maximum is checked at the endpoints and its sole stationary point $s=9/2$; derivatives use the actual square roots in~\eqref{eq:model}.
For the tail choose a nonnegative-coefficient polynomial $P_+$ bounding $|h|+F_{\max}|p|$, with degree $d\leq15$.
For $s\geq R=128$, $P_+(s)\leq P_+(R)(s/R)^d$.
Together with $\log(s/R)\leq(s-R)/R$, this yields
\begin{equation}
 \int_R^\infty |r|^2d\mu
 \leq\frac{R^{9/2}e^{-R}P_+(R)^2}
 {\Gamma(11/2)[1-(9/2+2d)/R]}.\label{eq:tail}
\end{equation}
The denominator is positive.
Saved cell and tail bounds were rounded upward to binary64; replay treats them as exact rational upper bounds.
The original interval producer is included unchanged as an algorithm reference; default replay recombines its saved results without regenerating cells.

Let $\epsilon_{0b}=\norm{\psi_5-\sum_jc_{bj}f_{bj}}$, evaluated from all sixteen exact basis coefficients rather than set to zero by truncation.
The spectral theorem and Duhamel's formula give, for every real $t$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{U_b(t)\psi_5-\widehat\psi_b(t)}
 \leq\epsilon_{0b}+|t|\sum_j|c_{bj}|r_{bj}=: \epsilon_b(t).\label{eq:duhamel}
\end{equation}
Thus the residual norms remain usable at the new time~\eqref{eq:time}; an old finite-time error or parameter box cannot simply be copied to that time.
Projecting onto normalized $\psi_5$ gives
\begin{equation}
 |k_b-\widehat k_b|\leq\epsilon_b(\tau_*),\qquad
 \widehat k_b=\sum_jv_{b0j}c_{bj}e^{i\lambda_{bj}\tau_*}.\label{eq:amplitude}
\end{equation}

\section{Sharp conditional fidelity and the complete instrument}
We use squared fidelity: for a pure target $\ket v$, $F_{\rm sq}(\rho,\ket v)=\bra v\rho\ket v$; for mixed targets it is squared Uhlmann fidelity.
The following elementary geometric estimate is proved here, not presented as new general mathematics.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sharp}
Let $D$ be unitary and $\norm{K-D}\leq\eta<1$.
For every normalized system--reference vector $\Psi$, successful application of $K$ has probability at least $(1-\eta)^2$ and conditional squared fidelity with $(D\otimes I)\Psi$ at least $1-\eta^2$.
The fidelity constant is sharp, including for contractions $K$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $v=(D\otimes I)\Psi$, $x=(K\otimes I)\Psi$ and $t=\norm x$.
Then $\norm{x-v}\leq\eta$ and $t\geq1-\eta>0$.
Expanding the squared distance gives
\begin{equation}
 \Re\langle v,x\rangle\geq\frac{1+t^2-\eta^2}{2}
 \geq t\sqrt{1-\eta^2},\label{eq:tangent}
\end{equation}
where the second inequality is $(t-\sqrt{1-\eta^2})^2\geq0$.
Dividing by $t$ and squaring proves the claim.
For sharpness let $a=1-\eta^2$ and take
\begin{equation}
 K=\begin{pmatrix}a&-\eta\sqrt a\\\eta\sqrt a&a\end{pmatrix},\qquad D=I.
\end{equation}
Then $(K-I)^\dagger(K-I)=\eta^2I$, $K^\dagger K=aI$, and the conditional squared fidelity for either coordinate vector is $a$.
\end{proof}
For a mixed logical/reference input, apply the lemma to a purification and discard the purifying system.
Monotonicity of squared fidelity gives the same bound for the normalized conditional mixed state.
The common radial source must initially be independent of that input; arbitrary radial/logical correlations are outside this preparation class.

To specify all outcomes, let $V\ket b=\ket b\otimes U_b(\tau_*)\ket{\psi_5}$.
The successful and failed operators $(I\otimes P)V$ and $(I\otimes Q)V$ have adjoint products summing to $V^\dagger V=I$.
Tracing radial outputs therefore defines a trace-preserving flagged instrument with completely positive branches.
The successful branch, including local correction, is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal S(\rho)=(C\otimes C)K\rho K^\dagger(C\otimes C)^\dagger.
\end{equation}
Its probability is the trace of this linear map; normalization is the explicitly conditional step.
No failures are omitted from the physical instrument.
Since $K$ is a contraction, its successful subnormalized map satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\mathcal S-\mathcal U_{\CZ}}_\diamond\leq2\eta.\label{eq:diamond}
\end{equation}
This bounds the successful branch, not a deterministic CZ on every run.
For input $\ket{++}$, $\CZ\ket{++}$ is maximally entangled.
The conditional logical output obeys
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal N(\rho_+)\geq F_{\rm sq}(\rho_+,\CZ\ket{++})-\tfrac12
 \geq\tfrac12-\eta^2,\label{eq:neg}
\end{equation}
Indeed, with $H=(\ket\Phi\bra\Phi)^{T_B}$ for the maximally entangled target, $\norm H_\infty=1/2$ gives
$F_{\rm sq}=\operatorname{Tr}(H\rho_+^{T_B})\leq\norm{\rho_+^{T_B}}_1/2=1/2+\mathcal N(\rho_+)$.
Here $\mathcal N(\rho)=(\norm{\rho^{T_B}}_1-1)/2$; the argument also covers mixed conditional outputs.

\section{A bounded instrument error box}
Let the actual normalized radial source and normalized rank-one readout mode each differ from $\psi_5$ by Hilbert norm at most $10^{-3}$.
Allow absolute timing error $|\Delta\tau|\leq10^{-3}$ and angle error at most $10^{-4}$ on each local phase gate.
The Hamiltonian parameters and exact invariant code are unchanged.
These are explicit control assumptions, not an inferred apparatus specification.
For the nominal source, $F_b(s)\leq Q_{\max}/s$ and
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\max}=q_{\rm bg}+2q_{\rm hi}=\frac{4862}{729},\qquad
 \langle y^{-4}\rangle_{\psi_5}=\frac4{63}.
\end{equation}
Using $A_b\psi_5=(11-F_b)\psi_5$ and $F_b\geq0$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \norm{A_b\psi_5}\leq M_1=
 \sqrt{121+\left(\frac{4862}{729}\right)^2\frac4{63}}
 <11.127631935535533.\label{eq:M1}
\end{equation}
Unitarity and the spectral theorem imply $\norm{[U_b(\Delta\tau)-I]\psi_5}\leq M_1|\Delta\tau|$.
A telescoping decomposition compares timing on the nominal source and handles source and readout changes separately by their norms.
Thus no graph-norm bound on an arbitrary norm-close perturbed source is needed for this bounded gate estimate.
Two local phase errors add at most twice their individual angle bound in operator norm, giving
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\eta\leq10^{-3}+10^{-3}+10^{-3}M_1+2\times10^{-4}
 <0.013327631935535532.\label{eq:extra}
\end{equation}
Adding this to the certified branch bound before its final display rounding yields
\begin{equation}
 \eta_{\rm rob}=0.20304819175570846<1.\label{eq:rob}
\end{equation}
Replay checks this comparison directly and tests positivity before applying success and fidelity formulas.
Table~\ref{tab:gate} gives the conservative lower bounds.
Lemma~\ref{lem:sharp} removes an unnecessary denominator $(1-\eta)^2$ from a cruder infidelity estimate; success is unchanged.
These norm-error assumptions do not transfer unbounded-density or density-RMS guarantees to arbitrary perturbed radial states.

\section{Reproduction, scope and provenance}
The packet supplies the exact archived trial and three complete residual partitions with hashes.
Running \texttt{python replay.py} requires only local Python packages and packet files, with no network or old project paths.
It verifies the tangent identity and sharp contraction algebra, input hashes, exact rational sums of saved residual cells and analytic tails, and the initial mismatch from all sixteen basis coefficients.
It then recomputes the frozen-time amplitudes, operator errors, robust box and sharper guarantees with 110-digit outward interval arithmetic.
The unchanged interval producer is included only as a minimal algorithm reference.
The underlying cellwise integrand bounds remain inherited certificates, not newly regenerated evidence.
This boundary between retained continuum control and freshly replayed consequences is part of the claim.

The protocol is a heralded entangling operation in the specified invariant model.
It does not establish a deterministic gate, universal loading/readout interface, spatial locality, a nonhomogeneous truncation theorem, global time optimality or a quantum-gravity completion.
Radial preparation/readout and local phases remain idealized controls with unquantified implementation costs.
The general lemma is an elementary geometric estimate; the scoped protocol and its continuum-supported frozen parameters are the construction reported here.
