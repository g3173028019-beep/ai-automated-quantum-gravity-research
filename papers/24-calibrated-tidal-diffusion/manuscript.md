## Result and its operational scope

In four-dimensional exact de Sitter (H>0), consider free linear Einstein gravitons in the Bunch–Davies state. Let e be a real symmetric tracefree comparator in a parallel tetrad of a central geodesic, with Frobenius norm one. Measure differential geodesic deviation by repeatedly calibrating short tracer baselines, not by indefinitely following a single expanding baseline. The compactly resolved stationary record has a strictly positive small-tube diffusion coefficient. Its pointlike-spatial limit in units c=ℏ=1 is
```
lim_(L→0) lim_(T→∞) Var(I_T)/T = κ² H⁵/(10π²) = 16 G H⁵/(5π), κ²=32πG.
```
This is a leading free-field variance of an integrated linear observable, not a drift of the background expansion, a graviton backreaction calculation, or a statement about statistics under repeated projective measurement.

## Clock, calibration, and endpoint transfer

In a parallel frame on a geodesic, prepare infinitesimal displacement ℓ n for a unit spatial vector n, and vanishing initial relative velocity in that frame at time s=0. Use synchronized proper clocks/Fermi simultaneous infinitesimal range; to first order in the separation, a local radar procedure reports the same displacement since both share the instantaneous inertial metric at the central event. Resolve its signed component X_n(s) on the transported n. The unperturbed Jacobi field is ℓ cosh(Hs)n. In a linear perturbation satisfying the vacuum Einstein equation with fixed Λ, the complete electric Riemann tensor in the transported orthonormal frame equals its constant-curvature background plus the electric Weyl tensor E. Consequently the calibrated first-order Jacobi equation is
```
(∂_s²−H²) δX_n(s) = −ℓ cosh(Hs) E_nn(s).
```
A shift of the actual central worldline, its tetrad, or its proper-time anchor acts only on the isotropic background constant-curvature tensor (whose tetrad components are unchanged), or on E which is already first order. Thus no omitted linear projection or clock effect can remove this right-hand side. The sign depends on the Riemann/Jacobi convention and is irrelevant to covariance.

Choose any smooth g of unit integral compactly supported in the interior of a fixed short acquisition interval (0,d). A calibrated readout is the negative of the integral of [g(s)/(ℓ cosh Hs)] times (δX_n''−H²δX_n). Integrate by parts twice to express it directly in terms of the range records with the compact weights [(g/cosh Hs)''−H²g/cosh Hs]/ℓ. The endpoint terms are zero and both homogeneous Jacobi solutions are annihilated, so small initial displacement and velocity corrections are not counted as curvature. Combining n along eigenvectors of e with its eigenvalues produces exactly the linear response ∫g(s)e:E(t+s) ds. The directional coefficients sum to zero. In particular the acquisition transfer has DC gain ∫g=1, not the double zero of an uncalibrated second difference of range. A different device that outputs only final range, relative velocity, or high-pass-filtered readout need not have this gain.

One may use a spatially distributed family of the same differential preparations within a small Fermi tube. Choose a smooth normalized spatial weighting on the static Fermi slice and transport the entire set of acquisitions by the central geodesic's de Sitter time-translation isometry. Center and emitter tetrads at each acquisition can be fixed by radial Fermi transport, and each local center follows its prepared timelike geodesic for that acquisition (it is not held on an accelerated static worldline while applying the free Jacobi equation). Use the timelike flow box generated by these short geodesic shots, so each finite-L acquisition is a compact smooth spacetime curvature test. Relative clock rates away from the central geodesic and the fixed rotations/boosts are built into the smeared test: they are smooth in L and tend to the correctly normalized on-axis transfer. Stationarity of the family is exact because the preparation, sampling, propagation and finish of each shot are jointly translated. This gives a finite-resolution record without treating an unsmeared field as a random variable. The operational elementary quantity is a *differential* geodesic displacement; a finite-pair range estimate has higher-order baseline corrections and is not identified as exactly Weyl at nonzero separation. The already finite spacetime tube resolution concerns the distribution of these calibrated differential shots.

## Independent two-helicity Wightman calculation on the geodesic

In flat coordinates η<0, a=−1/(Hη), use tensor modes g_ij=a²(δ_ij+γ_ij) and Frobenius-orthonormal polarization tensors ε^λ. With creation operators normalized by the usual d³k/(2π)³, the mode of γ_ij is
```
γ_k(η)=κH(1+ikη)e^(−ikη)/√(2k³).
```
This follows from the quadratic action (1/2κ²)∫a²[(γ_ij')²−(∂_aγ_ij)²] and flat positive-frequency normalization. Gauge-invariant orthonormal-frame electric Weyl is a^(−2) times the corresponding flat linearized Weyl of δ_ij+γ_ij. Direct subtraction of the flat linear Ricci trace from R_0i0j gives
```
E_ij=−(γ_ij''+Δγ_ij)/(4a²).
```
The mode is therefore iκH³η³ k^(3/2)e^(−ikη)/(2√2). In particular its integrand has no superhorizon k^(−3/2) metric mode. For unit e the average angular projection through the two TT helicities is 2/5: the angular-averaged polarization projector commutes with rotations on the five-dimensional STF representation and has trace 2. On the central geodesic x=0, η(t)=−e^(−Ht)/H, the Wightman distribution is
```
W(t,t') = κ²H⁶ η³η'³/(40π²) ∫₀^∞ k⁵ e^(−ik(η−η'−i0)) dk
        = −3κ²H⁶/(64π²) csch⁶[H(t−t'−i0)/2].
```
Both forms include the Wightman boundary-value prescription, not a time-ordered contact extension. The ratio of conformal-time factors gives the indicated hyperbolic sine; in changing the i0 representative one takes the common pulled-back positive-frequency boundary value. The flat limit of the proper spectrum below is κ² Ω⁵/(20π) for Ω>0, in agreement with the flat two-helicity E mode. The tensor power k⁵ in the first line removes the comoving IR cutoff at fixed finite spacetime smears before any long-time limit. No statement is made that a fixed comoving cutoff commutes with T→∞; such a cutoff would break the stationary scaling isometry.

For the Fourier convention S(Ω)=∫dτ e^(iΩτ) W(τ), use the conformally coupled four-dimensional scalar geodesic function W_c=−H²csch²[H(τ−i0)/2]/(16π²). Its Fourier spectrum is Ω/[2π(1−e^(−2πΩ/H))] by the elementary second-order pole contour (e.g. the image sum of shifted flat Wightman poles). With A=H/2, differentiation gives
```
(∂_τ²−16A²)(∂_τ²−4A²)csch²(Aτ)=120 A⁴ csch⁶(Aτ),
```
as an identity of the same boundary values. Thus
```
S_E(Ω)= κ² Ω(Ω²+H²)(Ω²+4H²)/[20π(1−e^(−2πΩ/H))].
```
It is nonnegative, satisfies S_E(−Ω)=e^(−2πΩ/H)S_E(Ω), and has the strictly positive zero-frequency limit quoted above. No inference of a nonzero value is made merely from thermality. For the on-axis time-resolved calibration the spectrum multiplies by |g̃(Ω)|², leaving DC unchanged.

## Exact finite-aperture coefficient from the transported acquisition

The finite-tube result is stronger than substituting the center-line spectrum. Introduce conformal variables for each point of a translated shot, measured relative to the central time anchor t:
```
η=−e^(−Ht) a/H,   x=e^(−Ht) y/H,   a>0.
```
Here a and y are dimensionless functions of acquisition time and prepared center label, not the FLRW scale factor. Both are independent of t because the whole shot is transported by the dilation isometry. Let dα w_L(α) be its compact sampling measure, incorporating normalized g and the spatial weights in preparation coordinates. Let P_λ(α,n) be the algebraic positive-frequency Weyl polarization contraction with the prescribed tracefree comparator in the shot's parallel tetrad. It includes the local boost/rotation relative to the conformal tetrad; the common electric curvature amplitude and its phase are stripped off. Magnetic components have the same amplitude scaling, so P_λ is independent of radial k. On axis P_λ=e:ε^λ for every acquisition time. The signed phase below is n·y+a with the convention exp(i k·x−i kη); its denominator is strictly positive in the tube.

Define the dimensionless shot amplitude
```
F_{λ,L}(z,n)= z³∫dα w_L(α) a(α)³ P_λ(α,n) exp[i z(a(α)+n·y(α))].
```
The physical one-mode amplitude, up to one common phase, is (κH³/(2√2)) k^(−3/2) F_{λ,L}(k e^(−Ht)/H,n), by the TT Weyl mode above. For each direction, F=O(z³) at z→0, uniformly as the tube shrinks. The derivative of a+n·y along each acquisition is bounded away from zero by uniform timelikeness and the finite shot duration. Time integrations by parts then show uniform rapid decrease as z→∞. Thus F belongs to both L¹ and L² in log z, uniformly, and the Wightman function is its scale autocorrelation:
```
W_L(τ)=(κ²H⁶/8) Σλ ∫ dΩ_n/(2π)³ ∫₀^∞ dz/z
                           F_{λ,L}(z e^(−Hτ),n) conjugate(F_{λ,L}(z,n)).
```
This is a compact-acquisition result. Its DC value is the squared zero Mellin moment, not a point spectrum multiplied by an assumed time-only transfer:
```
S_L(0)=(κ²H⁵/8) Σλ ∫ dΩ_n/(2π)³ |∫₀^∞ F_{λ,L}(z,n) dz/z|²
      =(κ²H⁵/2) Σλ ∫ dΩ_n/(2π)³
       |∫dα w_L(α) P_λ(α,n)[1+n·y(α)/a(α)]^(−3)|² .
```
For the second line introduce e^(−εz) in the z integral. Its kernel is 2/[ε−i(a+n·y)]³, tending to −2i/(a+n·y)³; exchange at ε>0 and take the uniform limit since a+n·y is bounded away from zero on the compact tube. The first line follows by Fubini in log z and H dτ = −dlog(z e^(−Hτ)). All positions, velocities and tetrads enter through the finite-aperture integral; independent tracer directions contribute at the amplitude, not probability, level. On axis denominator is one and the polarization is constant, recovering κ²H⁵/(10π²) without inserting the previously known pointlike spectral density. For smooth even spatial preparation weights about the central geodesic, shot data depend smoothly on the initial center in a uniform compact flow box; linear terms in its spatial offset integrate to zero. Thus S_L(0)=S_E(0)+O(κ²H⁵ (HL)²) for a fixed dimensionless shot protocol, with a finite coefficient depending on the protocol. Without symmetric centering continuity already establishes strict positivity at sufficiently small L.

## Finite-tube limit and actual long record

For the short locally geodesic shots with smooth spatial averaging of size L, write W_L(τ) for the stationary covariance between two acquisitions (already temporally smooth). On every compact proper-time τ interval, W_L converges in distributions, and against smooth cutoffs, to the temporally filtered on-axis W. One way to check the pullback is the positive-frequency microlocal condition: null wavefront covectors cannot annihilate the timelike geodesic tangent. For compact acquisition windows, the family of spatial localization approximations lies in the corresponding wavefront-controlled test topology. In the explicit conformal mode representation the same conclusion follows from nonstationary phase after temporal smearing: along uniformly timelike local worldlines the conformal phase derivative for all spatial k directions is bounded away from zero on fixed compact shot windows, so repeated integration by parts suppresses k^5 uniformly for bounded center-clock offsets. Finite rotations, clock Jacobians and short-baseline calibration converge smoothly to their central values.

For |τ| larger than a fixed constant depending on the two shot widths and tube radius, W_L has a uniform integrable exponential tail as L→0. Indeed in flat coordinates the central time-translation rescales both η and x about x=0. In the fixed small physical Fermi tube, |x|/|η| is bounded strictly below one, uniformly including the short shots. For sufficiently unequal scales, every angular denominator |η−η'−khat·(x−x')| is bounded below by a fixed positive multiple of the larger conformal scale. The two linear curvature amplitudes carry η³η'³; performing the radial k⁵ integral gives the sixth inverse power of that denominator. Magnetic Weyl components, needed for small off-axis boosts, have the same η³k^(3/2) mode scaling from a^(−2)∂γ′ and bounded angular factors. Orthonormal boosts and smooth sampling are uniformly bounded. The result is O(exp(−3H|τ|)), uniformly for sufficiently small L. Combining local convergence with this tail bound gives S_L(0)=∫W_L→S_E(0). In particular the coefficient is strictly positive for every sufficiently small nonzero spatial resolution in this calibrated family.

Let I_T be the integral of the record against smooth switching with a unit plateau of proper length T and fixed endcaps. For a Hermitian measurement the variance uses the real symmetrized covariance; the Wightman odd part cancels and both spectra agree at zero. The overlap of two translated plateau windows divided by T tends to one at every fixed time difference and is uniformly bounded; use integrability from the smooth shots and the uniform tail. Hence Var(I_T)/T→S_L(0) at fixed L, with the subsequent L→0 limit displayed above. For small fixed L, exponential tails also bound the endcap correction by O(1). For future plateau windows anchored at a fixed finite past time, this conclusion also has a uniform low-comoving-momentum estimate. The omitted k<k_IR two-shot kernel is bounded in magnitude by C k_IR^6 exp[−3H(t+t′)], from the O(z³) mode amplitudes and the dk/k measure. Integration over the switched plateau then bounds the absolute variance error by O(k_IR^6), uniformly as its future endpoint tends to infinity. This does not imply stationarity at fixed cutoff or uniformity for windows whose past anchor tends to minus infinity. This variance is fluctuations in a time-integrated finite-resolution tidal record. It is neither UV switching noise nor a coordinate secular drift from freezing metric modes.

[Kouris](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0107064) gives the de Sitter Weyl two-point tensor and [Dai–Pajer–Schmidt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02011) develops Fermi-frame cosmological response. The result here uses its own TT projection and the calibration transfer to obtain the exact zero-frequency coefficient and the long-time finite-tube limit. It differs from the [mean local Hubble-rate analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.11124).
