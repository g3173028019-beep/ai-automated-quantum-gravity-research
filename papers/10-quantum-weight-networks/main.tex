\begin{abstract}
A four-level quantum control cell can realize a compact symplectic algebra rather than the full special-unitary algebra.
We study a positive-weight family whose two weighted logical controls and transverse update generate exactly $\mathfrak{spin}(5)$.
The weight direction selects its six-plus-four symmetric decomposition and a polarized Clifford trace.
A vector-polarization bond between two independently controlled cells generates $\mathfrak{spin}(10)$ on one chiral sixteen-dimensional module.
This exceptional pairing fails to scale: every connected network of at least three independently controlled cells, with the specified vector or clock-bivector bonds independently available, generates the full $\mathfrak{su}(4^n)$.
We prove the network extension by a finite Clifford-algebra induction, with exact integer Pauli calculations supplying the small-system bases.
A separate iid collective limit gives three canonical pairs with an explicit polarization-noise bound.
Its diagonal commutator error vanishes in state root mean square but has constant nonzero operator norm.
The phase Hamiltonian is a transverse Curie--Weiss model with spectator degrees of freedom; it supplies compatible one-site polarizations but does not establish an interacting-Gibbs fluctuation theorem.
The result is a finite control construction with a completed proliferation obstruction and precise collective-limit boundaries.
\end{abstract}
\section{Questions and scope}
Restricted orthogonal and symplectic dynamical algebras are established in quantum control~\cite{Schirmer2002,DAles2008}.
Clifford realizations make their representations explicit, while generic added interactions can restore full controllability.
This makes an exceptional two-cell algebra scientifically useful only after its behavior under repetition has been determined.
We ask three connected questions: which algebra follows from the positive-weight control family; whether its chiral pairing extends across a network; and which large-block variables survive if independent microscopic control is replaced by a specified product-state fluctuation limit.

The component group-theoretic identities are standard.
The contributions are an explicit weight-parameterized control family and its update restriction, a complete connected-network classification for two bond types, and an exact distinction between state and operator-norm convergence in its collective construction.
The local algebra is also a Euclidean Cartan carrier, and finite embedding algebras have a long history~\cite{Snyder1947,Yang1947,Chandrasekharan1997}.
These motivations do not identify the present variables with a spacetime, a coframe, a matter field or gravitational dynamics.
All controls in the network theorem are independently available real Hamiltonian directions; the theorem does not concern a single fixed homogeneous Hamiltonian.
\section{A weighted four-level dynamical algebra}

Let $X,Y,Z$ denote the Pauli matrices and $I$ the $2\times2$ identity.  On
$\mathcal H=\mathbb C^2_{w}\otimes\mathbb C^2_{\ell}$ define
\begin{equation}
G=\frac32 I-\frac12 Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,2),\qquad
H_x=G\otimes X,\quad H_y=G\otimes Y,\quad H_q=X\otimes I .
\label{w:eq:seed}
\end{equation}
The first factor is called the weight factor and the second a logical factor.  We use the Hermitian Lie bracket
\begin{equation}
\Hbr{A}{B}=\frac{[A,B]}{i}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact weight-update closure]\label{w:thm:closure}
The real Hermitian Lie algebra generated by \eqref{w:eq:seed} has dimension ten and Pauli support
\begin{equation}
\mathcal B=\{IX,ZX,IY,ZY,XI,IZ,ZZ,YX,YY,YZ\}.
\label{w:eq:basis}
\end{equation}
It is centerless and perfect.  For the anti-Hermitian representation $T=iH$, the space of antisymmetric forms satisfying
\begin{equation}
T^{\mathsf T}\mathcal J+\mathcal JT=0
\end{equation}
is one-dimensional and contains the nonsingular witness
$\mathcal J=iZ\otimes Y$, with $\det\mathcal J=1$.  The compact algebra is therefore
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak g\cong\mathfrak{usp}(4)\cong\mathfrak{so}(5).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Starting with $H_x,H_y,H_q$, repeatedly append every nonzero bracket that increases the exact flattened matrix rank.  The process stops at the ten independent strings in \eqref{w:eq:basis}.  Exact structure constants give zero center and a ten-dimensional derived algebra; the Killing form in the computed real basis is negative definite.  Solving the displayed linear equations for a general $4\times4$ antisymmetric matrix gives a one-dimensional null space and the stated nonsingular witness.  Thus the defining module is symplectic, the algebra is compact and ten-dimensional, and the standard compact classification identifies it with $\mathfrak{usp}(4)$, equivalently $\mathfrak{so}(5)$.
\end{proof}

The classification is not inferred from dimension alone: the invariant form, compact Killing signature, center, and derived algebra are separately checked.  Notice also what the theorem does not say.  Finite-dimensional Hermitian evolution produces a compact unitary group; no noncompact Lorentzian real form follows by relabeling generators.

\section{The weight as Cartan involution and trace selector}

Introduce five Hermitian matrices
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_1=Y\otimes I,\quad
\Gamma_2=X\otimes X,\quad
\Gamma_3=X\otimes Y,\quad
\Gamma_4=X\otimes Z,\quad
\Gamma_5=Z\otimes I
\label{w:eq:gammas}
\end{equation}
and bivectors
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{AB}=\frac{[\Gamma_A,\Gamma_B]}{2i},\qquad 1\leq A<B\leq5.
\label{w:eq:sigma}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Clifford carrier]\label{w:prop:clifford}
The matrices \eqref{w:eq:gammas} obey
\begin{equation}
\{\Gamma_A,\Gamma_B\}=2\delta_{AB}I_4,\qquad
\Gamma_1\Gamma_2\Gamma_3\Gamma_4\Gamma_5=I_4.
\end{equation}
The ten matrices $\Sigma_{AB}$ have rank ten and span the algebra of Theorem~\ref{w:thm:closure}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct Pauli multiplication proves the anticommutators and ordered product.  Flattening the ten bivectors gives matrix rank ten.  Expressing them and \eqref{w:eq:basis} in the Pauli basis shows equality of the two spans.
\end{proof}

The traceless direction of the original weight is $S=\Gamma_5$.  Define
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak h=\operatorname{span}\{\Sigma_{ab}:1\leq a<b\leq4\},\qquad
\mathfrak p=\operatorname{span}\{\Sigma_{a5}:1\leq a\leq4\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic symmetric pair]\label{w:thm:split}
The weight direction $S$ has a six-dimensional centralizer in $\mathfrak g$ and grades it as
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak p,\qquad
[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak h]\subset\mathfrak h,\quad
[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak p]\subset\mathfrak p,\quad
[\mathfrak p,\mathfrak p]\subset\mathfrak h.
\label{w:eq:symmetricpair}
\end{equation}
Here $[S,\mathfrak h]=0$ and $\{S,\mathfrak p\}=0$.  Hence the generating weight itself selects the compact symmetric pair $SO(5)/SO(4)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All assertions follow by exact multiplication of \eqref{w:eq:gammas}--\eqref{w:eq:sigma}.  The dimension of the centralizer is the nullity of the linear map $A\mapsto[A,S]$ restricted to the ten-dimensional span; it is six.
\end{proof}

The same $S$ also selects an invariant tensor.  Let $\epsilon_{1234}=1$ and
\begin{equation}
\rho_\nu=\frac14(I_4+\nu S),\qquad -1\leq\nu\leq1.
\label{w:eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Its eigenvalues are $(1\pm\nu)/4$, each with multiplicity two.

\begin{proposition}[Polarized trace identity]\label{w:prop:trace}
For $a,b,c,d\in\{1,2,3,4\}$,
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Tr}(S\Sigma_{ab}\Sigma_{cd})&=-4\epsilon_{abcd},\label{w:eq:eps}\\
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_\nu\Sigma_{ab}\Sigma_{cd})
&=\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}-\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}-\nu\epsilon_{abcd}.
\label{w:eq:weightedtrace}
\end{align}
The $S$-polarized mixed $\mathfrak h$--$\mathfrak p$ and $\mathfrak p$--$\mathfrak p$ traces vanish.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{w:eq:eps} is the Clifford trace of four distinct coset directions with $S=\Gamma_5$ and the orientation fixed by the ordered product in Proposition~\ref{w:prop:clifford}.  Expanding \eqref{w:eq:rho} gives \eqref{w:eq:weightedtrace}; direct exact traces cover repeated indices and the vanishing sector statements.
\end{proof}

The epsilon contraction in \eqref{w:eq:weightedtrace} is the algebraic ingredient used in MacDowell--Mansouri-type broken curvature actions \cite{MacDowell1977,Wise2010}.  Those actions are not derived here.  The audited statement is narrower: one microscopic operator both generated the finite algebra and selected its symmetric pair and broken trace.

\section{A two-cell bond and chiral \texorpdfstring{$\operatorname{Spin}(10)$}{Spin(10)}}

Consider two copies of $\mathcal H$.  The uncoupled local algebra is generated by
\begin{equation}
L_{AB}=\Sigma_{AB}\otimes I_4,\qquad
R_{AB}=I_4\otimes\Sigma_{AB}.
\end{equation}
Add the polarization bond $B=S\otimes S$.  Its orbit under independent left and right local actions consists of the span
\begin{equation}
M_{A\dot B}=\Gamma_A\otimes\Gamma_{\dot B},\qquad A,\dot B=1,\ldots,5.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Selective two-cell closure]\label{w:thm:spin10}
The left, right, and cross subspaces have dimensions $10$, $10$, and $25$, respectively, and satisfy
\begin{align}
[\mathfrak g_L,\mathfrak g_R]&=0,&
[\mathfrak g_L\oplus\mathfrak g_R,\mathfrak m]&\subset\mathfrak m,&
[\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m]&\subset\mathfrak g_L\oplus\mathfrak g_R.
\end{align}
Both $S\otimes S$ and $\sum_A\Gamma_A\otimes\Gamma_A$ have a 25-dimensional local orbit.  Either bond therefore generates a 45-dimensional compact algebra with decomposition
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak{spin}(10)
=\bigl(\mathfrak{spin}(5),\mathbf1\bigr)
\oplus\bigl(\mathbf1,\mathfrak{spin}(5)\bigr)
\oplus(\mathbf5,\mathbf5).
\label{w:eq:spin10decomp}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Exact flattened ranks give $10+10+25=45$.  Projecting every pairwise bracket onto the three subspaces gives zero residual.  Successive adjoint action of the 20 local generators on either bond spans all 25 cross matrices.  The bracket pattern, compactness, dimension, and standard symmetric decomposition identify the algebra as $\mathfrak{spin}(10)$; the decomposition itself is standard \cite{Slansky1981}.
\end{proof}

The paired Hilbert space has dimension 16.  Its chirality can be read without assuming a branching table.  Define
\begin{equation}
H_1=\Sigma_{12}\otimes I,\quad H_2=\Sigma_{34}\otimes I,\quad
H_3=I\otimes\Sigma_{12},\quad H_4=I\otimes\Sigma_{34},\quad
H_5=S\otimes S.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Direct chirality certificate]\label{w:thm:chirality}
The five $H_k$ commute, obey $H_k^2=I_{16}$, and satisfy
$H_1H_2H_3H_4H_5=I_{16}$.  For $s\in\{\pm1\}^5$, the joint projector
\begin{equation}
P_s=\prod_{k=1}^5\frac{I_{16}+s_kH_k}{2}
\end{equation}
has rank one if $\prod_ks_k=+1$ and rank zero if $\prod_ks_k=-1$.  The commutant of the local product algebra is scalar.  Thus the paired space is one irreducible chiral 16 of $\operatorname{Spin}(10)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The commuting and product identities follow exactly from Proposition~\ref{w:prop:clifford}.  Expanding all 32 projectors gives the stated ranks.  Solving the commutant equations for a general $16\times16$ matrix gives dimension one, proving irreducibility.
\end{proof}

This closure is selective.  A bivector dot product, a rotation-only dot product, or a coset-only dot product generates the full $\mathfrak{su}(16)$ under independent local actions, whereas a single vector bridge and the two bonds in Theorem~\ref{w:thm:spin10} generate the 45-dimensional algebra.  Selection of the bond is therefore substantive; it is not a generic property of two four-level systems.

\section{Exact multicell proliferation}

The two-cell polarization bond is unusually small: independent local $\mathfrak{spin}(5)$ actions plus $S_1S_2$ close on $\mathfrak{spin}(10)$.  We now test whether repeating the bond produces a spatial chain.  In the Pauli frame used above, one cell has the ten-string basis
\begin{equation}
\{IX,ZX,IY,ZY,XI,IZ,ZZ,YX,YY,YZ\},
\label{c:eq:paulibasis}
\end{equation}
with polarization $S=ZI$ and clock bivector $C=YZ$.

Every $n$-qubit Pauli string is represented by a binary vector $(x|z)\in\mathbb F_2^{2n}$.  Two strings anticommute precisely when their symplectic product is one; their nonzero commutator is proportional to the string encoded by their binary sum.  Closing a set under this rule is therefore exact and avoids numerical matrix ranks.  This binary representation is standard in Pauli-algebra analysis and quantum control \cite{Cuypers2026}.

\begin{theorem}[Three-cell proliferation obstruction]\label{c:thm:proliferation}
Allow the ten local generators \eqref{c:eq:paulibasis} independently on each cell of an open chain.  Add nearest-neighbor bonds of either type $S_rS_{r+1}$ or $C_rC_{r+1}$.  The exact Lie-closure dimensions are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c@{\qquad}r@{\qquad}r@{\qquad}r}
\toprule
cells & $S_rS_{r+1}$ & $C_rC_{r+1}$ & $\dim\mathfrak{su}(4^n)$\\
\midrule
1 & 10 & 10 & 15\\
2 & 45 & 255 & 255\\
3 & 4095 & 4095 & 4095\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thus three independently controlled cells with either raw bond generate all of $\mathfrak{su}(64)$.  In particular, the two-cell $\mathfrak{spin}(10)$ closure does not iterate as a chain law.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Encode all local strings and bonds as binary symplectic vectors.  Repeatedly add the binary sum of every anticommuting pair until no new vector appears.  The exact cardinalities are those in the table.  For three cells the cardinality $4095=4^6-1$ is the number of all nonidentity Pauli strings on six qubits, which span $\mathfrak{su}(64)$ as Hermitian generators.
\end{proof}

The theorem rules out a tempting interpretation: the paired $\operatorname{Spin}(10)$ carrier cannot simply be placed on every spatial link while retaining independent cell controls.  It may still serve as an on-site representation, but a scalable low-energy sector would need a symmetry, a gap, or a projection that changes the effective algebra.

\section{The nested \texorpdfstring{$SO(3)$}{SO(3)} decomposition}

Using the Clifford matrices and bivectors already fixed above, set
\begin{equation}
J_i=\frac12\epsilon_i{}^{jk}\Sigma_{jk},\qquad
K_i=\Sigma_{i4},\qquad E_i=\Sigma_{i5},\qquad C=\Sigma_{45},
\label{c:eq:jekc}
\end{equation}
where $i,j,k\in\{1,2,3\}$.  We again use $\Hbr{A}{B}=[A,B]/i$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact finite phase-space algebra]\label{c:prop:finite}
The ten operators in \eqref{c:eq:jekc} are independent and decompose as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{10}=\mathbf3_J\oplus\mathbf3_K\oplus\mathbf3_E\oplus\mathbf1_C
\end{equation}
under the rotations generated by $J_i$.  Their nonzero brackets are
\begin{align}
\Hbr{J_i}{J_j}&=2\epsilon_{ij}{}^kJ_k,&
\Hbr{J_i}{K_j}&=2\epsilon_{ij}{}^kK_k,&
\Hbr{J_i}{E_j}&=2\epsilon_{ij}{}^kE_k,\nonumber\\
\Hbr{K_i}{K_j}&=2\epsilon_{ij}{}^kJ_k,&
\Hbr{E_i}{E_j}&=2\epsilon_{ij}{}^kJ_k,&
\Hbr{K_i}{E_j}&=2\delta_{ij}C,\label{c:eq:brackets}\\
\Hbr{C}{K_i}&=2E_i,&
\Hbr{C}{E_i}&=-2K_i,&
\Hbr{C}{J_i}&=0.\nonumber
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exact flattened rank is ten.  Substitution of the Clifford matrices and Pauli multiplication yields every bracket in \eqref{c:eq:brackets}.  The $J$ brackets then display two vectors and one scalar under the common $SO(3)$.
\end{proof}

This is a compact Yang/Snyder-type phase-space algebra, not a new abstract algebra.  Our use of it is tied to the specific cell: $E_i$ are simultaneously part of its weight-selected Clifford coset, and $C$ anticommutes with the weight polarization introduced next.

\section{Double polarization and the quantum central limit}

Let
\begin{equation}
S=\Gamma_5,\qquad
\rho_{\nu,\mu}=\frac14(I+\nu S+\mu C).
\label{c:eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Since $S^2=C^2=I$ and $\{S,C\}=0$, this two-parameter state can be analyzed exactly.

\begin{theorem}[Positive double-polarized cell]\label{c:thm:positive}
The spectrum of \eqref{c:eq:rho} is
\begin{equation}
\frac{1\pm\sqrt{\nu^2+\mu^2}}4,
\end{equation}
each eigenvalue occurring twice.  Hence $\rho_{\nu,\mu}\geq0$ exactly when
$\nu^2+\mu^2\leq1$.  For generic nonzero $\nu,\mu$, the common centralizer of $S$ and $C$ inside $\mathfrak{spin}(5)$ is the three-dimensional algebra generated by $J_i$.  The one-cell moments are
\begin{align}
\langle C\rangle&=\mu,&
\langle J_i\rangle=\langle E_i\rangle=\langle K_i\rangle&=0,\nonumber\\
\frac12\langle\{E_i,E_j\}\rangle&=\delta_{ij},&
\frac12\langle\{K_i,K_j\}\rangle&=\delta_{ij},\label{c:eq:moments}\\
\left\langle\Hbr{K_i}{E_j}\right\rangle&=2\mu\delta_{ij}.&&
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The anticommuting involutions give $(\nu S+\mu C)^2=(\nu^2+\mu^2)I$, proving the spectrum and positivity disk.  The centralizer is the null space of the two simultaneous adjoint maps restricted to the ten-dimensional algebra and has dimension three.  Direct traces of \eqref{c:eq:rho} with the matrices in \eqref{c:eq:jekc} give \eqref{c:eq:moments}.
\end{proof}

For $N$ independent cells in the product state $\rho_{\nu,\mu}^{\otimes N}$, define the fluctuation operator
\begin{equation}
F_N(A)=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{r=1}^N\bigl(A_r-\langle A\rangle I\bigr).
\end{equation}
Assume $\mu>0$ and set
\begin{equation}
e_i^{(N)}=\frac{F_N(E_i)}{\sqrt{2\mu}},\qquad
k_i^{(N)}=\frac{F_N(K_i)}{\sqrt{2\mu}}.
\label{c:eq:scaled}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Three-pair CCR contraction]\label{c:thm:qclt}
In the product-state quantum central limit, the six variables in \eqref{c:eq:scaled} converge in moments to a regular CCR algebra with
\begin{equation}
\Hbr{k_i}{e_j}=\delta_{ij},\qquad
\Hbr{e_i}{e_j}=\Hbr{k_i}{k_j}=0.
\label{c:eq:ccr}
\end{equation}
Their symmetric covariance is diagonal and
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}(e_i)=\operatorname{Var}(k_i)=\frac1{2\mu}.
\label{c:eq:variance}
\end{equation}
Consequently positivity imposes the kinematic bound
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}(e_i)=\operatorname{Var}(k_i)
\geq\frac1{2\sqrt{1-\nu^2}}
\end{equation}
at fixed $\nu$ and $\mu>0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The product-state noncommutative central-limit theorem maps centered one-site observables to a CCR fluctuation algebra whose symplectic form is the expectation of their commutator \cite{Goderis1989}.  Equation \eqref{c:eq:moments}, together with the normalization in \eqref{c:eq:scaled}, gives \eqref{c:eq:ccr} and \eqref{c:eq:variance}.  Maximizing $\mu$ on the positivity disk at fixed $\nu$ gives the last inequality.
\end{proof}

At finite $N$ the same scaling gives
\begin{equation}
\Hbr{k_i^{(N)}}{e_j^{(N)}}
=\delta_{ij}\frac{C^{(N)}}{N\mu},\qquad
\Hbr{e_i^{(N)}}{e_j^{(N)}},\ \Hbr{k_i^{(N)}}{k_j^{(N)}}
=O\!\left(\frac{J^{(N)}}N\right).
\end{equation}
The contraction is therefore visible directly in the finite algebra when $C^{(N)}/N\to\mu$ and $J_i^{(N)}/N\to0$ in the chosen state.  Calling $(e,k)$ ``triad'' and ``extrinsic curvature'' would be only a proposed interpretation: no spatial index, derivative, or Hamiltonian constraint has yet been constructed.

\section{A dynamical origin for the polarized state}

The state \eqref{c:eq:rho} need not be inserted by hand.  Define collective sums
$S_N=\sum_rS_r$ and $C_N=\sum_rC_r$ and the permutation-invariant Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
H_N^{\mathrm{pol}}=-\frac{J}{2N}S_N^2-hC_N,\qquad J,h>0.
\label{c:eq:meanfieldH}
\end{equation}
The thermodynamic-limit mean-field functional for the order parameter
$\nu=\langle S_N\rangle/N$ is
\begin{equation}
f(\nu)=\frac J2\nu^2-\frac1\beta
\log\!\left[4\cosh\!\left(\beta\sqrt{J^2\nu^2+h^2}\right)\right].
\label{c:eq:free}
\end{equation}
Such thermodynamic limits and variational descriptions are standard for mean-field spin and quantum Curie--Weiss models \cite{Bianchi2003,Bapst2012}.

\begin{theorem}[Mean-field double-polarization phase]\label{c:thm:phase}
Let $r=\sqrt{J^2\nu^2+h^2}$.  The derivatives and self-consistency equations are
\begin{align}
f'(\nu)&=J\nu\left[1-\frac Jr\tanh(\beta r)\right],\label{c:eq:fp}\\
f''(0)&=\frac Jh\left[h-J\tanh(\beta h)\right],\label{c:eq:fpp}\\
\nu&=\frac{J\nu}{r}\tanh(\beta r),\qquad
\mu=\frac h r\tanh(\beta r).\label{c:eq:self}
\end{align}
The continuous mean-field boundary is
\begin{equation}
J\tanh(\beta h)=h,
\end{equation}
with an ordered solution when the left side exceeds $h$.  At zero temperature and $J>h$, the two symmetry-related ordered states have
\begin{equation}
\nu=\pm\sqrt{1-(h/J)^2},\qquad \mu=h/J.
\label{c:eq:zeroT}
\end{equation}
At finite temperature, $\sqrt{\nu^2+\mu^2}=\tanh(\beta r)<1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \eqref{c:eq:free} exactly to obtain \eqref{c:eq:fp}--\eqref{c:eq:fpp}.  The one-site effective Hamiltonian is $-J\nu S-hC$; because $S$ and $C$ anticommute, its Gibbs state is of the form \eqref{c:eq:rho} and yields \eqref{c:eq:self}.  In the zero-temperature ordered phase, nonzero $\nu$ implies $r=J$, giving \eqref{c:eq:zeroT}.
\end{proof}

Equation \eqref{c:eq:meanfieldH} is spatially ultralocal: it explains the internal block phase, not curvature or transport.  The mean-field result also does not prove stability under finite-range interactions in three dimensions.

\section{What collective symmetry protects---and what it does not}

Every polynomial in the collective sums of a block commutes with the action of all within-block permutations.  Hence each permutation sector is exactly invariant under \eqref{c:eq:meanfieldH} and under any collective-polynomial extension.

\begin{proposition}[Exact permutation-sector protection]\label{c:prop:perm}
Let $P_\lambda$ project onto any isotypic sector of the permutation action on $(\mathbb C^4)^{\otimes N}$.  If $H$ is a polynomial in collective sums $\sum_rA_r$, then
\begin{equation}
[H,P_\lambda]=0,\qquad (I-P_\lambda)HP_\lambda=0.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each collective sum is invariant under permutation conjugation.  So is every polynomial in such sums.  The invariant operator is block diagonal in the permutation-isotypic decomposition, proving both identities.
\end{proof}

This exact statement is weaker than gravitational protection.  Suppose $P_N$ denotes a proposed low-energy/constraint projector and $\widehat C_I^{(N,a)}$ microscopic constraints at cell number $N$ and spatial resolution $a$.  A viable continuation must establish both
\begin{align}
\left\|P_N\left(\Hbr{\widehat C_I}{\widehat C_J}
-\widehat f_{IJ}{}^K\widehat C_K\right)P_N\right\|&\leq\epsilon_{N,a},
&\epsilon_{N,a}&\longrightarrow0,\label{c:eq:closurebound}\\
\left\|(I-P_N)\widehat C_I P_N\right\|&\longrightarrow0.
\label{c:eq:leakage}
\end{align}
Product-state fluctuation estimates suggest $O(N^{-1/2})$ kinematic corrections, while a smooth centered spatial difference is often $O(a^2)$.  Balancing them would suggest $N^{-1/2}=o(a^2)$, for example $N\gg a^{-4}$.  This scaling is a heuristic target, not a theorem: inverse operators, correlations, and nonsmooth states may invalidate it.


\section{Interpretation and reproducibility}
The positive-weight family gives an economical finite control realization of familiar compact structures.
Its symmetric pair and epsilon trace are the algebraic ingredients used in known broken-curvature constructions~\cite{MacDowell1977,Wise2010}; neither an action nor a Lorentzian real form follows from the control algebra.
The paired chiral representation is likewise a representation result, not a derivation of fermion statistics or a field theory.
The all-network theorem makes the principal obstruction exact: unrestricted local controls and these raw bonds cannot define a spatial network whose algebra remains the exceptional paired algebra.
A constrained model, restricted control set, symmetry sector or low-energy projection would require additional hypotheses and a separate analysis.

The iid collective theorem is a valid alternative kinematic construction.
It has three nondegenerate CCR pairs for fixed positive clock polarization, but it supplies no spatial derivative, Hamiltonian constraint, gravitational projector or continuum propagation.
Exact permutation-sector protection remains weaker than protection of an intended physical constraint sector.
In particular, Proposition~\ref{prop:error} prevents an $N^{-1/2}$ state-fluctuation estimate from being presented as the operator-norm closure bound written above.
The heuristic spatial double scaling is not a consequence of the results proved here.

The portable script \texttt{code/verify.py} reconstructs the one-cell closure with exact matrices, checks the invariant symplectic form, Clifford and polarized trace identities, all thirty-two paired Cartan projectors, the six-variable moments, the displayed brackets, the mean-field derivatives and the update counterexample.
It also performs integer symplectic closure for one through four cells with both bonds.
All output is written under \texttt{outputs/}; the rational Pauli definitions and algorithm are included in the script and \texttt{inputs/model.json}.
Closing a Pauli set under repeated adjoint actions of the original generators suffices by Jacobi; every nonzero bracket is the binary XOR of two anticommuting strings.
